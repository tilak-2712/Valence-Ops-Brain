the no-show problem: at Aurilueur a no-show is usually a **missed session inside an already-paid

### 1.3 The proposal that was sent (5 Sep)

*"Aurilueur Esthetic Clinic — Enquiry and Appointment Systems Proposal"*, 9 pages. Structure: where
this picks up → how enquiries are handled today → **what this looks like as the clinic grows** →
what we would build → what your team does instead → how we would start → what it costs → next step.

**Three systems, switched on one at a time:**

1. **Enquiry handling on every channel** — WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger and the website form into one
   view; instant reply at any hour; captures treatment, concern and availability; sends a booking
   *request* to the desk with the conversation attached; hands over the moment a human replies.
   *Where it stops:* it does not confirm the appointment and it does not quote prices or give medical advice.
2. **CRM entry and a weekly report** — writes every enquiry, reply, booking and outcome into Neodove;
   records response latency; one weekly page of enquiries, sources, speed, consultations booked and attended.
3. **Reminders, missed appointments and free slots** — confirmation and day-before reminder the patient
   can reply to; morning list of who has not confirmed; same-day call raised for no-shows; next-session
   offers for patients mid-course; a waitlist that gets messaged when a slot frees; day-rearrangement
   messaging with replies collected. *Where it stops:* it never picks or books a time, the rep does.

**Explicitly not in scope, and said so in the document:** quote follow-up and weight-loss programme
retention (deferred to when there are numbers to judge them by), and managing availability at all.

**Commercials:** ₹90,000 listed, founding-client waiver −₹50,000, **₹40,000 one time** ·
**₹18,000 per month** from the month the first system goes live · both exclusive of GST and other
applicable taxes · **valid until 30 September 2026.** Platform charges are billed to the clinic
directly on accounts in their name: WhatsApp Business API under ₹1,500/month, conversational-AI usage
₹2,000–₹6,500/month at their volume. Timeline: **3–4 weeks from access to the first system live.**

**The close:** a live WhatsApp number they can message like a patient, at any hour, before deciding
anything. Plus two questions carried into the next call — whether the no-shows are first consultations
or sessions inside a course, and whether "30–40%" means enquiry-to-consultation or enquiry-to-treatment.
Next step asked for: an online onboarding walkthrough, two dates from them.

### 1.4 Judgment settled on this deal (from `taste-n-judgement.md`)

- **Sized by integration surface, not prestige.** Single branch, one CRM, one channel, one inbox owner
  is the *cheapest* build and the most likely to complete, which is what a first case study needs.
  Tilak's "not a high-profile portfolio client" framing points the wrong way.
- **Discount the scope, never the rate.** Cutting ₹40,000/month as a headline rate would travel to
  every future client and undermines the number already in writing to SkinFit. Fewer systems for a
  smaller monthly is honest; the same systems cheaper is a discount that follows you around.
- **Never say "overstaffed."** The frame is absorption: who handles the next 200 enquiries a month
  when the ads scale. The cost of not fixing is **the next headcount**, which is his own arithmetic.
- **The machine decides when to call; it never replaces the call.** Text fires only on "no answer",
  at a different time of day. The most valuable feature is knowing when to stop — which is exactly
  what produced their public complaint about pushy messaging.
- **Do not automate a decision the clinic makes by improvisation.** Doctor availability lives in the
  rep's head. Rep decides, system types.
- **A scope built from a self-report intake will automate what the clinic already does well.** Adi's
  three original systems map one-to-one onto the three form answers where Shabeeb described an
  existing process. Build scope from the evidence layer; use the form to confirm or contradict it.

### 2.3 The proposal that was sent (30 Aug)

*"SkinFit Wellness — Patient Conversion Proposal"*, 12 pages, scoped explicitly to Drish's own words.

**Three parts of one system:** (1) **speed to lead** — instant useful reply on any channel at any hour,
capturing concern, treatment, area, duration, timeline, availability, escalating anything clinical;
(2) **qualification** — KAI brought into the WhatsApp journey, the free assessment used as the opening
hook and the score returned into the thread; (3) **the bridge** — every WhatsApp conversation tied to
one searchable patient record with the attribution chain preserved end to end.

**Section 11, priced and committed separately: call recording and transcripts.** Exotel telephony, a
virtual number, Sarvam speech-to-text with speaker separation, transcripts attached to the patient
record. The document states plainly what it commits SkinFit to: **desk-bound calling from a softphone,
every call, every day** — and names the risk honestly, that if desk-bound calling will not hold on a
clinic floor the record looks complete while being partial, which is worse than not having it.

**Deliberately out of scope, matching Drish's own view:** booking automation beyond the link, no-show
recovery, quote follow-up, review generation — all "once there is data behind them."

**Commercials:** **₹35,000 build + ₹20,000/month** for the core, **+₹15,000 build and +₹7,000/month**
for the call channel = **₹50,000 build · ₹27,000 monthly**, exclusive of GST. Usage billed at cost with
no markup (Meta/WhatsApp, Sarvam, Exotel). Stated as **firm figures, not estimates**, with the one
moving part named: if the KAI handoff works differently from what their team expects.

**Next step asked for:** an introduction to whoever is building the KAI website, to settle the handoff
directly, **explicitly with no meeting needed** — and the timing argument that a handoff is far easier
to design into a site being built than retrofitted after launch.
