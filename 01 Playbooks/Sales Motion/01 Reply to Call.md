# 01 — Reply to Booked Call

**Owns:** Stage 0 and the sending half of Stage 1. From the moment a positive reply lands to the moment a call is on the calendar.
**Voice rules:** `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §3 banned list and §4 tone apply to every line below, unchanged. Enquiries not leads. Patients not conversions. No "growth", "scale", "funnel", "ROI". No superlatives. No guaranteed numbers.

---

## The clock

**≤4 business hours to first response. No exceptions, including "I need to think about how to answer this."**

If the full packet can't go out in that window, a holding message goes out instead. A holding message that names a date is a commitment; silence is a verdict. You sell response speed — a three-day gap is a live demonstration that you don't have it.

**Who responds:** the POC on the Notion tracker row. If they're unreachable for more than 4 hours, the other one responds and tags the row. A shared inbox nobody owns is how the first one got dropped.

---

## Triage — three types

Read the reply once and classify before writing anything. Most replies are Type B and most people mishandle Type B.

### Type A — Curiosity
> "Good observation." / "Interesting." / "How did you find this out?" / "We've actually been thinking about this."

**What they're asking:** is there more where that came from?
**Trap:** dumping everything you found. The one-pager already spent your best fact. Answering with three more facts turns a conversation into a report and there's nothing left for the call.
**Move:** answer their literal question honestly and shortly, add exactly one new piece of texture, then offer the walkthrough and two slots.

**Template A:**
> Thanks — and happy to say how.
>
> I messaged your WhatsApp on a Tuesday afternoon as a patient asking about [treatment], and timed what came back. The rest is public — your ads, your Google reviews. Nothing clever.
>
> What I can't see from outside is the part that actually matters: what happens to an enquiry after someone replies to it. That's the bit I'd want to look at with you.
>
> I've put together a short walkthrough of how we work and what a first month looks like — about six minutes, sending it across now so you're not hearing it cold on a call.
>
> Would Thursday 4pm or Friday 11am work for 30 minutes? No pitch — I mostly want to understand how enquiries move through your clinic.

### Type B — Credential check
> "Send us your company profile." / "Share your details / pricing." / "Which clinics do you work with?" / "Send a proposal."

**What they're asking:** are you real, and will this waste my time?
**This is the one that broke last time.** Two traps, and they pull in opposite directions:
- Treating it as a document request → you send a PDF, wait, and the reply goes cold. A document request with no date attached is a stall, whether or not they meant it as one.
- Refusing the document and pushing straight to a call → reads as evasive to someone who just asked a reasonable question.

**Move:** send the packet *and* two slots, in the same message. The packet answers the credential question. The slots stop the stall. Neither works alone.

**Template B:**
> Of course — attaching two things.
>
> The first is a short walkthrough (six minutes) of how we work, what the first month looks like, and what we'd need from you.
> The second is a document covering the same in writing, including what we charge and what we don't touch.
>
> One thing worth saying upfront rather than letting you find out later: we're early. We don't have a portfolio of clinic case studies to send you. It's the two of us, and the clinics we take on now get worked on directly by us. That's the honest trade, and it's laid out on page 3.
>
> If it reads like it's worth 30 minutes — Thursday 4pm or Friday 11am? I'd want to understand how enquiries actually move through your clinic before saying anything about what we'd do.

**On the "we're early" line:** it goes in the first message, not held back for the call. Volunteered honesty reads as confidence. The same fact extracted under questioning reads as something you were hiding. This is the whole positioning and it's cheapest to deploy here.

### Type B-variant — "What's your pricing?"
Do not send a price list. Do not dodge either.

> The pilot is free — two to three weeks, on one specific thing, so you can see whether it works before any money is involved. If it does and you want to carry on, setup is ₹20,000 and it's ₹40,000 a month after that.
>
> I'd rather not quote anything more specific until I've seen how your enquiries actually move — otherwise I'd be pricing a guess. That's most of what the 30 minutes is for.

### Type C — Ready
> "Call me." / "When are you free?" / "Let's talk."

**Move:** book first, in one line, then send the packet as pre-work. Don't make them read anything before they get a time.

**Template C:**
> Thursday 4pm or Friday 11am — either works, whichever suits you.
>
> Sending a short walkthrough across now (six minutes) so we don't spend the call on what we do. I'd rather spend it on how enquiries move through your clinic.

### The holding message — when the packet can't go same-day
> Thanks [name] — genuinely glad it was useful.
>
> Sending you two things by tomorrow evening: a short walkthrough of how we work, and what a first month looks like in writing.
>
> In the meantime — would Thursday 4pm or Friday 11am work for 30 minutes?

Two sentences, a named date, a named slot. Nothing else. This message costs four minutes and is the difference between a warm reply and a lost one.

## Slot mechanics

**Offer two slots, not a calendar link.** A link asks a busy clinic owner to do work. Two named times ask for a yes or a counter-offer, and either answer moves things forward. If they counter with a third time, take it without negotiating.

**Pick slots a clinic owner can actually take.** Avoid morning consultation hours and the evening rush. Mid-afternoon Tuesday–Thursday tends to be the quiet stretch. If the clinic's own mystery-shop data shows when they reply fastest, use that — it's a free signal about when the founder is free.

**Offer the in-person drop-in first, the call as the fallback.** *(Corrected 2026-08-13 — `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §4 previously recorded this as a proven pattern; it is UNPROVEN, zero meetings and zero calls held. Sound reasoning, no evidence — don't state it as a pattern to a clinic.)* *"I'm around Indiranagar most weeks — happy to swing by instead if that's easier."* Offer it alongside the call, never instead of it.

**Send a calendar invite immediately once a slot is agreed.** With a title that says what it is — "Valence Ops — 30 min, how enquiries move through [clinic]" — and the video link in the body. An unconfirmed verbal slot is not a booking.

## Nudges when they go quiet

Different from cold-outbound follow-up. This person already replied, so the tone shifts from earning attention to being easy to say yes to. Two nudges, then park.

**Nudge 1 — +3 days**
> [Name] — no rush on this. If the timing's off, say so and I'll check back in a couple of months instead of pestering you.
>
> If it's just been a busy week: Tuesday 3pm or Wednesday 4pm?

**Nudge 2 — +7 days after that**
> Assuming this isn't the right time — closing the loop so it's not sitting in your messages.
>
> The walkthrough's still there if it's useful, and the WhatsApp finding stands whether or not we ever speak. Happy to pick it up whenever.

Then stop. `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §4: accept declines cleanly, no push, no "can I ask why."

## When they ask something you can't answer

**"Which clinics have you done this for?"**
> None yet, in this exact form. That's the honest answer and I'd rather give it than dress it up. What I can do is show you the system running on a demo number — you can message it yourself and watch what it does. I'd rather you test it than take my word for it.

**"How is this different from [agency they've used]?"**
> Most of what's sold to clinics is about getting more enquiries in. This is about what happens to the ones you already get — after someone messages, after they book, after they've had a consult and haven't come back. If your problem is enquiry volume, we're genuinely the wrong people.

**"Can you just send a proposal?"**
> I can, but it'd be a guess — I'd be proposing something for a clinic I've only seen from outside. Give me 30 minutes to understand how enquiries actually move, and what I send afterwards will be specific to you and worth reading.

**"Is this AI? Will patients know they're talking to a bot?"**
> Fair question and it's the one most owners ask. Nothing pretends to be a person. Where something is automated, it reads as a clinic message, and the moment a patient says anything a system shouldn't answer, it stops and hands it to your coordinator. I'd rather show you than describe it — it's in the walkthrough.

## Log it

Append to `files/REPLY_LOG.csv` the same day:

```
date_replied,clinic,reply_type,hours_to_our_response,packet_sent,personalized_video,
call_booked,call_held,findings_sent,pilot_agreed,stage_lost_at,their_words
```

Per `MEMORY.md` §4, a row per send isn't expected. **A row per reply is** — replies are rare enough that logging each one costs almost nothing, and it's the only data that will tell you where this motion actually breaks.
