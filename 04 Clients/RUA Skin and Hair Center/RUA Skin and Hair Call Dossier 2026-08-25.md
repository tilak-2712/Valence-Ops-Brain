**Entry SKU:** **Qualification + scoring upgrade**, extended into booking conversion
**Routing row (§3):** sits between two rows, and the honest read is a blend.
*Ads ✓ · greeting-only auto-reply · no qualification questions* → Qualification + scoring upgrade
supplies the mechanism, with the variation that **their auto-reply is better than greeting-only. It
offers a menu. The menu just isn't wired to anything.** But one rotating creative is effectively
no-ads, so *No ads · website ✓ · high reviews* → **"Your reputation markets for free. Your inbox
kills what it earns"** supplies the emotional frame. Take the mechanism from the first row and the
language from the second. Do **not** take that row's "slow or no response" trigger — §4.1 killed it.
**Status:** `provisional` — designed, and now backed by a first-party shop, but never run in the field
**Explicitly rejected:** Dead-lead reactivation (§0.2). Response speed (§4.1). Any Meta-Pixel/ads angle (§9).

### The one sentence — UPDATED after §5a

> *"On the 26th of July you published a post with your device vendor about RF microneedling for acne
> scars, and it ended with 'DM us to book your consultation.' I did. I asked what you offer for acne
> and acne scars. The answer didn't name a single treatment, and it didn't include your booking link
> — which is live on both your Google listings. Whoever answers that inbox doesn't know what you
> publish, and doesn't have the tools you've already bought."*

**Rebuttal test:** the only two replies available are *"the person on WhatsApp doesn't see what we
post"* and *"they weren't trained on it."* **Both concede the sale.** The medical rebuttal that
killed this hook in §4.1 — *"we don't diagnose over text"* — no longer works, because they published

### The supporting sentence, if you need a second beat

> *"Your auto-reply offers three choices, your website form asks which of five treatments a patient
> wants, and your Google listing has a Book button. Three systems collecting the same answer, and the
> person who finally replies still has to ask 'what treatment you want to know.'"*

### Why this wedge and not the branch gap

The 93%-vs-32% asymmetry is the more *emotionally* powerful fact, but it has a true rebuttal — *"Indiranagar is newer, we're still staffing it."* Correct, and it deflates the hook. So: **lead with the wiring, let them raise the branch gap themselves.** Section 7 is built to make that happen.

## 7. The call

### 7.1 Opening — earn the right in 30 seconds

Do not lead with a finding. Lead with a fact only someone who looked would know.

> "Before anything else — you replied to 78 of your 84 reviews at Ramamurthy Nagar, most of them the same day. One of them was a four-star with no text and you still wrote back to ask what would have made it five. I've been through about forty clinics in Bangalore this month and I haven't seen that twice. So I want to be clear I'm not here to tell you that you don't follow up. You obviously do."

Then the turn:

> "What I want to ask you about is what happens *before* the patient is in the chair. Because I think the follow-through you've built inside the clinic stops at the front door."

**Optional second beat, if the room is warm** — this is the "earned, not bought" line from §5b:

> "The other thing I noticed is you're not really buying your patients. One ad on Meta, nothing on Google, no Practo. A hundred-odd five-star reviews and a post a day is what's bringing people in. Which I respect — but it also means when a message goes cold, there's no ad budget quietly filling the gap. You just lose that one."

### 7.2 The questions — in this order

Each is designed so the honest answer does the selling. **Ask, then stop talking.**

**Q1 — the opener that opens them up**
> "When someone messages the Indiranagar WhatsApp at seven in the evening, who sees it first?"

*Why it works:* there is no good answer. Any true answer — a name, "one of us", "the front desk till 8" — hands you the dependency. If they say *"one of us checks"*, you have three MD dermatologists doing inbox triage. Say nothing. Let it sit.

**Q2 — the branch question, asked as curiosity not accusation**
> "You opened Indiranagar around March. Does it feel like it's running the way Ramamurthy Nagar was running at the same age?"

*Why it works:* this is the thing they already worry about at night. You are not telling them; you are inviting them. **Whatever they say next is the real brief for the engagement.** If they volunteer the gap, you never have to cite the 32%.

**Q3 — the wiring question**
> "When a patient fills in the form on your site and ticks 'Hair Transplantation' — where does that tick end up?"

*Why it works:* they almost certainly don't know. The form is agency-built. The answer is either "I'd have to check" (which is the finding) or a destination they can name (which tells you what to integrate with).

**Q4 — the one that lands the wedge without you asserting it**
> "Last one on this. If I message you and ask what you do for acne scars — what's supposed to happen next?"

*Why it works:* they will describe the ideal — assess, recommend, book them in. Then you show the screenshot. The gap between what they just described and what actually happened is a gap **they** created, out loud, ten seconds earlier. That is the moment.

**Q4b — the follow-up that closes the trap. Ask it only after they've answered Q4.**
> "And the RF microneedling — the one you posted about with Primelaze in July. Is that something the front desk can talk about, or does that only come up once the patient's in front of one of you?"

*Why it works:* it is a **genuine operational question**, not a gotcha, and it is the one that produces the admission. Either answer wins:
> — *"They should be able to"* → then show the screenshot. They couldn't.
> — *"No, that's a doctor conversation"* → *"Then every acne-scar enquiry has to wait for one of you to be free. How many of those come in a week?"*

This is also where you name the asset without pricing it: **you invested in something specific for acne scars, and the first line of response can't refer to it.** Do not say what it cost or what it needs to earn (§9).

**Q5 — the money question, once they're open**
> "February you did twenty-eight reviews. March, twelve. Since then it's been four to six a month. What changed in February?"

*Why it works:* it proves you read the data, not the brochure. And it surfaces whatever push they ran and stopped — which is either a campaign that worked and wasn't sustained, or a launch. Either answer is useful.

**Q6 — the ad question, asked as a routing question not a marketing one**
> "You've got one ad running on Meta since the fifteenth. When someone taps 'Send message' on it — which phone does that land on?"

*Why it works:* you genuinely do not know the answer, and neither, probably, do they. Instagram's button goes to Ramamurthy Nagar, the website's goes to Indiranagar. If the ad is a third destination, that's a fourth front door and the map draws itself. **Keep it about routing.** Do not drift into ad performance — §9, hard kill #7.

**Q7 — the qualifier you actually need**
> "Roughly how many enquiries land across both branches in a month — WhatsApp, calls, Instagram, all of it?"

*Why it works:* this is **hard kill #3** (under ~20/month) and it is not determinable any other way. Ask it plainly. Also probe average treatment value against **hard kill #4** (₹25,000) — they do hair transplants and Cosmelan, so it likely clears, but do not assume it.

### 7.3 Showing the screenshot — the exact framing

Only after Q4. Hand it over and use these words:

> "This is my own message to Indiranagar. I want to point at one line — not the timing, the timing's on me, I wrote at half nine at night and you were shut."

*(Conceding this yourself is the whole move. It removes their only defence and buys the rest.)*

> "It's this: 'Kindly book a consultation.' That's the right answer. But there's a Book button live on both your Google listings right now, and it wasn't sent. And it isn't on your website either — I checked all five pages. So the one time somebody was told to book, the thing you built for booking wasn't in the room."

**Then the beat that lands it — have the 26 July post open on your phone next to the screenshot:**

> "And this is the part I keep coming back to. A month before I wrote to you, you published this — with Primelaze, about RF microneedling for acne scars, ending 'DM us to book your consultation.' I did exactly that. I asked about acne scars. And the reply doesn't have the words 'RF microneedling' anywhere in it.
>
> I'm not saying anyone did anything wrong. I'm saying the person answering that inbox has no idea what you published, and no way to send me the booking link. That's not a people problem."

**Two images side by side is the whole pitch.** Their own post, and their own reply. Say less than you want to.

Then, if you want the second beat:

> "Same page — your 'Call Us Now' button dials one-two-three-four-five-six-seven-eight-nine-zero. On all five pages. Please check that tonight regardless of whether we ever work together."

**Give them (a) free and unconditionally.** It costs nothing, it is worth real money to them, and it converts the call from a pitch into a debt.

### 7.4 The close

Route to the standing offer — **free audit, paid implementation**. Do not quote on this call.

> "Here's what I'd suggest. Let me map every route a patient can take to reach you — Instagram, the website, both Google listings, both WhatsApp numbers — and show you where each one ends up and what gets lost in between. That's free and it takes me a couple of days. If what comes back is boring, you've lost nothing and you've still got the phone number fixed."
