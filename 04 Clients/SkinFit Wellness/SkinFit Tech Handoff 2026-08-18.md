# SkinFit Wellness — Technical Handoff

**For:** Adi and Maddy · **From:** Tilak and Pratham · **Date:** 18 August 2026
**Status of the lead:** first meeting held and positive. Awaiting a second conversation with the founder. The proposed next step is a no-cost audit, which is where the technical side starts.

**Read this before the audit.** It is the complete context on this lead, including the things we are not sure about. Nothing here has been said to the client except where marked.

**Epistemic labels used throughout:** `confirmed` (we verified it ourselves or they stated it) · `provisional` (designed or observed once, not proven) · `hypothesis` (a reasoned guess, do not act on it as fact).

### What the first mystery shop found `confirmed`

A WhatsApp enquiry about hair treatment went to the clinic's listed number. A person replied straight away, with no delay and no bot. When details were asked for in writing, the answer was that typing it all out was difficult and the conversation was pushed to a phone call. **The hair concern was never asked about, and nothing was recorded in the chat.**

That is the pattern the whole engagement is built on: fast to greet, nothing captured.

### One thing in that one-pager was wrong, and we corrected it in the room

The document stated as a confirmed finding that **no paid campaigns were running on Meta or Google.** That was true when we checked on 1 August. **They went live on Meta on 3 August.** Gino is the person running those campaigns, so it was the one claim in the document he could personally falsify.

Tilak raised it himself at the start of the meeting rather than waiting for it to be spotted. It landed well, but it is the reason the standing rule now exists: **re-verify anything time-sensitive before a document leaves.**

### Second mystery shop, still running `confirmed`

We sent an enquiry **through the ₹499 Messenger ad on Saturday 16 August at 9:38 PM.** The ad was live and taking clicks at that hour. **As of the meeting on Monday it had not been answered.**

Note this tested a **different inbox** from the first shop. The first went to their listed WhatsApp number; this one went into Messenger, which is where the ad money actually lands.

## 4 · Their marketing engine — what generates the volume

All verified directly in the Meta Ad Library on 16–17 August. `confirmed`

- **Around 20 active ads**, running since 3 August. Roughly 8 video and 12 static.
- **Launch waves** on 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 16 August. They were still adding new creatives during our research, so this is an actively managed account, not set-and-forget.
- **Every single ad opens a Messenger conversation.** None send anyone to the website.
- **Some ads open a form inside Messenger.** Gino says the submitted details "land with the front desk" but could not say in what inbox or what format.
- **Tripwire offers:** ₹499 first laser session, ₹1,499 first medical-grade peel, ₹1,999 hydrafacial.
- **Instagram** `@skinfitwellness.in`, about 1,838 followers, posting consistently since 11 June with roughly a two to three day gap. Reels since late June have reached between 14K and 91K.

**Why the ad structure matters to us:** a tripwire model only pays back if the person actually turns up and then buys a package. That makes show-up rate and follow-up the load-bearing parts, not just response speed.

### A volume discrepancy worth resolving in the audit

`hypothesis` — do not repeat either number to the client.

From the ad structure and typical Bangalore messaging costs, we estimated they should be generating somewhere in the region of **27 to 50 conversations a day**. **Their own tracking, which Gino showed us, records around 10 enquiries a day.**

Three possible explanations, and the audit should settle which:
1. Actual spend is lower than we estimated.
2. Their sheet counts qualified or logged enquiries, not raw message threads.
3. **A large share of Messenger conversations are never recorded anywhere.** If Meta's conversation count is materially higher than their enquiry sheet, the difference is enquiries that arrived and left no trace.

If (3) is even partly true, it is the single most valuable finding available in this engagement.

## 7 · What we have already committed to in writing

**This section matters most for Adi.** The client document `04 Clients/SkinFit Wellness/SkinFit Wellness - Enquiry Handling Review.pdf` has been prepared for sending and contains six explicit commitments. **Every one needs your confirmation before it goes out.** If any cannot be honoured as written, tell us and we change the document, not the practice.

1. **Patient records stay where they are.** We work on incoming messages, not medical records. Their patient system stays theirs.
2. **Nothing automated ever gives medical advice.** It captures what someone is asking and routes it to their team. It never answers clinical questions or speaks in a doctor's name.
3. **We only message people who contacted them first**, within what WhatsApp and Meta permit. No purchased lists, nothing that puts their number at risk.
4. **Every message is logged and readable by them.** Nothing goes out unseen.
5. **Access is granted and withdrawn by them** at any time. Numbers, pages and accounts stay in the clinic's name.
6. **The data stays theirs.** We operate the system on their instruction and never take a copy of their patients elsewhere.

**Point 3 is the one to check hardest.** It assumes we are on the official WhatsApp Business API through a BSP. If we are on anything else, that sentence is false in a document going to a founder.

**The audit scope we have promised:**
- We look at how enquiries have been handled over **the past two weeks or more**, across Messenger and the ad forms, Instagram, WhatsApp, the website form, and the calls the front desk makes.
- **The message threads themselves are enough.** We explicitly told them there is no report to prepare and nothing to export, because they may not have structured data.
- About **thirty minutes** of their team's time, plus read-only access to the enquiry history and the Meta account.
- **No patient medical records at any stage.**
- They get a written map of where enquiries are lost and how much sits at each point, ranked. Theirs to keep either way.

## 8 · What the audit has to establish

In rough priority order.

1. **Where the Messenger ad-form submissions actually arrive**, and whether anyone opens that inbox. If they are sitting in Meta's Leads Center unretrieved, that is immediate, recoverable money.
2. **The true conversation count versus their logged enquiry count** (see §4). The gap, if there is one, is the headline finding.
3. **Time to first reply**, per channel and per hour of day.
4. **How many enquiries were attempted more than once**, and what happens to someone who is quoted and goes quiet.
5. **What hours enquiries actually arrive**, against the hours the desk is staffed.
6. **The exact point at which people stop responding.**
7. **Whether a booking can be written into their existing system**, or whether we can only request a slot. This materially changes what we can promise.
8. What someone finds when they search the clinic after seeing an ad.

## 9 · Open technical questions — Adi

These are not audit findings. They are things we need answered about **our own capability** before we can scope anything.

**Channel**
- Do we support **Messenger** and **Instagram DM**, or only WhatsApp? Their entire ad spend lands in Messenger, and we built for WhatsApp.
- Can we ingest **Meta lead-form submissions**?
- One inbox across all channels, or one channel done properly? What does unified cost us?
- Integration will need access through **their Meta Business Manager**, which Gino controls. He is an ally, which helps.

**Platform legitimacy and rules**
- Official WhatsApp Business API through a BSP, or something else?
- What is genuinely possible inside and outside the **24-hour messaging window**, on both Messenger and WhatsApp? This directly limits reminders, no-show recovery, quote chasing and any reactivation of older enquiries.

**Booking**
- Can we write a confirmed appointment into their practice software, or only hand a slot request to staff?
- **Two locations** means branch routing and two sets of availability.

**Attribution — potentially our strongest card**
- Click-to-Messenger ads pass a **referral parameter identifying the originating ad**. Can we capture it and carry it through to a booking?
- If yes, we can tell them which ad produced which booked patient, which is exactly what Gino cannot show today. It also lets booking events be fed back to Meta as the conversion signal, so the algorithm optimises toward people who book rather than people who chat.

**Cost, data and delivery**
- Per-conversation cost at their volume, and **who fronts it**.
- Where does data physically sit, and are we processor or fiduciary under DPDP?
- **Language:** ads are in English, replies will not all be. Hindi and Kannada handling.
- What is a realistic **first visible artifact**, and how quickly?
- Which parts of the build are genuinely ours, and which are commodity? We need to know what not to describe in detail.

## 10 · What not to assume, and what not to say

- **Do not treat the practice-software name as known.** It was heard once in a meeting and is not confirmed.
- **Do not quote the enquiry volume as a hard number** to anyone outside the team. Roughly ten a day is their recollection of their own sheet, not a measurement.
- **Do not repeat our ad-spend estimate to the client.** It is a `hypothesis` built from ad structure. They know the real figure.
- **Do not mention Ritesh Sachdev**, or any of the company-register or LinkedIn research on Israni and Gino.
- **Never say the front desk is broken.** Two named people do that job and they are calling enquiries back, which most clinics in this market do not do at all. Everything we say frames the gap as structural.
