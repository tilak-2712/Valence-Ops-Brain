# Run-sheet — Gino Tharakan / SkinFit Wellness · Mon 18 Aug 2026

**Tilak + Pratham** · SkinFit Koramangala 4th Block · Read with `Meeting Brief SkinFit 2026-08-18.md`

> **This is beats, not a script.** The project already paid for this lesson: a written script narrated aloud sounded "recited like a poem." Anything memorised sounds memorised. Four lines below are worth having close to word-perfect because precision matters in them. Everything else is a beat — hit it in your own words.
>
> **Gino is experienced, not junior.** Don't explain performance marketing to him, don't over-frame, don't handle him. Senior people are repelled by technique and respond to being treated as an equal.

---

## 1 · The four things that must happen

Everything else is negotiable. These are not.

1. **The correction** — the one-pager's "no campaigns running" line, raised by you, early
2. **The complementary position** — we don't do ads, creative or websites
3. **The carryable sentence** — said once, clearly, so it survives the trip to Israni
4. **A date** — on something, before you leave the room

If you get all four and nothing else, the meeting worked.

---

## 2 · Where to be adaptable — read the room, pick the branch

You will know which branch you're in within ten minutes of Q1.

| Branch | Signal | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **A · The funnel is tight** | He answers everything crisply, someone owns the inbox, replies are fast | **No speed wedge — don't force one.** Pivot to measurement: they still can't tie a booking back to an ad. Sell attribution, not response time |
| **B · He doesn't know** *(most likely)* | "I'd have to check" on where leads land, who replies, what converts | **Never press.** → *"No worries — who would know? Worth getting them in?"* His blank becomes your introduction to the person who owns the funnel |
| **C · He's scoping you** | Lots of questions about how it works, little given back | Stop giving. Questions only. **Hold the demo.** Close on a date with a decision-maker |
| **D · He wants it for the agency** | "Could you do this for our other clients?" | *"Let's prove it on this one clinic first."* Do not get excited in the room |
| **E · Dr. Ruby or Israni walks in** | — | **Audience change.** Stop the funnel detail. Go high-level and short: what you saw, what it costs, the free audit, a date. Two minutes, not twenty |

**Branch B is the likeliest.** He's a creative director — where Messenger form submissions land is a media buyer's job, not his. Him not knowing is a finding, not a failure.

## 3 · Before you walk in

- **Arrive 10 minutes early and sit in reception.** Not waiting — observing. Who's waiting, is anyone on the phone, is the desk person typing on a handset. You are here to discuss the front desk; watch it first. **Never mention what you saw unless it's flattering.**
- **Roles: Tilak leads and asks. Pratham notes and runs the demo.** One questioner at a time — two-on-one turns a conversation into an interview.
- **On the phone:** demo number tested that morning · screen recording as fallback · the one-pager open.
- **Leave behind:** deck, proposal, pricing, contract.
- **Confirm he's actually coming in person** — his profile says Mumbai.

---

## 4 · Opening beats — about 5 minutes

**Beat 1 — human first.** Two or three minutes. Not business.

**Beat 2 — the compliment, and make it specific.** Name an actual line from their ads:

> *"The men's one — 'built for men, not adapted for them.' And the acne scars one, texture versus pigment. Whoever's writing those knows what they're doing."*

Leaves room for him to claim it, which he will. Generic praise reads as technique; a quoted line reads as attention.

**Beat 3 — take the pitch off the table.** ~*"I'm not here to pitch you. I mostly want to understand how enquiries actually move through the clinic. If there's something useful at the end, good — if not, fine."*

That sentence buys every question that follows.

---

## 5 · The four lines worth getting right

Say the rest however it comes out. These four carry weight.

**① The correction** — after he's finished his agenda, before you ask about the funnel. Say this one close to verbatim; the dates are the point.

> *"One thing in that document is out of date and I'd rather flag it than have you spot it. It says no paid campaigns are running — that was true when we checked on the 1st. You went live on the 3rd. Which is part of why I wanted to meet in person. Can you walk me through what the ads are doing now?"*

It proves you re-check instead of sending templates, it shows you know their activity, and it's your bridge into the ads conversation.

**② The position** — when he asks what you do. Lead with what you don't.

> *"We don't do ads, creative or websites — you're good at that and we'd be useless at it. We do the part after someone clicks: the enquiry gets answered, qualified, booked, reminded, and chased if they don't show."*

**③ The carryable sentence** — once, near the end.

> **"You've built a good machine up to the click, and nothing after it."**

**④ The close.**

> *"The audit we mentioned — thirty minutes, no cost, and you get the map back either way. Can we put it in the diary this week?"*

## 6 · The questions, by what each one does

### Open here — these two, in this order

**Q1 · *"What made you reach out?"***
Ask before you say anything about yourselves. Whoever states their agenda first frames the meeting. **Listen for his word** — leads, messages, follow-up, bookings — and use his word for the rest of the meeting.

**Q2 · *"How did you end up working with the clinic — how long has that been?"***
Sounds like small talk. It's the org chart. He'll volunteer the agency shape and probably Israni, without you revealing you checked.

### Questions that prove you did the work

Specificity is the credibility. A generic question about "your marketing" proves nothing; naming their own creative proves you looked.

- *"Walk me through what happens when someone taps the ₹499 ad — from the tap to them in the chair."*
- *"Everything's pointing at Koramangala. Is Richmond Road deliberate, or not ready yet?"*
- *"You've done other medical brands — Healthium, ortho. How does a skin clinic compare?"*

### Questions that build the diagnosis

- **"Those forms that open inside Messenger — where do the submissions land? Who picks them up?"** ← the highest-value question you have
- *"Who actually replies — front desk, you, someone else? On a phone or a computer?"*
- *"How many enquiries a day now versus before the ads went live?"*
- *"What happens to someone who asks a price and then goes quiet?"*

> **On the Messenger forms:** Meta parks those in the **Leads Center**. If nothing pulls them out, they sit in a dashboard nobody opens. `hypothesis` — but if he doesn't know, that's found money and it came from him.

### Questions that make the case for you

Don't argue the problem. Ask the question whose answer is the problem.

- **"Are you reporting to the clinic on leads, or on bookings?"** ← he will feel his own exposure as he answers
- *"Is the ₹499 crowd the kind of patient the clinic wants, or a lot of price-shoppers?"*
- *"Of everyone who books, roughly how many actually turn up?"*
- *"What does a good 90 days look like for you?"*

### The question that decides the next step

- **"Who else would need to be in the room for something like this?"**

Asked as a practicality, not a probe. It's the single most valuable answer you can leave with.

### The reflect-back — only after he describes manual handling

> *"So if it's thirty to fifty conversations a day and every one is typed by hand — that's someone on a phone for hours with patients sitting in front of them."*

Arithmetic, not accusation. **This must not sound like criticism of their staff.**

## 7 · What you take home either way

Even if they never buy, these are worth the trip. Get them deliberately.

| # | What to come away with | Why it's worth more than the deal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **His exact words for the problem** | `MEMORY.md` §2 — felt-problem language is what makes outbound land, and we've been guessing at it. One marketing professional who talks to clinic owners weekly is worth many copy revisions |
| 2 | **Whether the conversion gap is universal or SkinFit-specific** | This is the thesis the whole business rests on. Ask him plainly: *"Is this normal across the clinics you've worked with?"* |
| 3 | **Where Meta form leads actually land in practice** | We've never seen inside a live clinic funnel |
| 4 | Benchmarks — cost per conversation, CPL, show-up rate | Every future spend estimate stops being a guess |
| 5 | What the agency **can't** do, from someone who does this daily | Validates or kills our positioning |
| 6 | What clinics complain to their agency about | Free wedge research |
| 7 | Who decides in a clinic like this | Fixes our targeting across the whole list |
| 8 | Whether agencies would ever partner on this | Sizes the channel, without proposing anything |

**Number 1 and 2 are the real prize.** Write them down in the car, in his words, before they fade.

## 9 · Objections

| He says | You say |
|---|---|
| *"Who are you? Who else do you work with?"* | Early, plainly, first. Client #1 gets two founders who can't afford to fail; client #40 at an agency gets a junior account manager. **This is the question Sapphire asked and where that thread died — say it out loud tonight** |
| *"How much?"* | *"Depends what the audit finds. That part's free either way."* |
| *"We could build this ourselves."* | *"Probably — you've got the team. Question is whether it's worth your build time versus having it next week."* Don't fight it |
| *"Can you do our ads / site / content?"* | **No, plainly.** Refusing it is what makes everything else credible |
| *"Can you white-label under us?"* | *"Let's prove it on this one clinic first. Then that conversation's much easier for both of us."* |
| *"Send me a proposal."* | *"Happy to — can we hold twenty minutes to go through it, so it doesn't sit in an inbox?"* |
| *"I'll discuss internally and get back."* | *"Makes sense. What would you need from me to make that easy?"* → *"Shall we hold twenty minutes Thursday either way, so it doesn't drift?"* |
