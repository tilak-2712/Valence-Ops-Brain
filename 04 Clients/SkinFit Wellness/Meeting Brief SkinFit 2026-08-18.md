## 4 · Gino: the actual risk, and the actual prize

**Tilak's "he'll extract and leave" instinct is right but mis-specified.** A freelancer extracts to do the work himself. **An agency extracts to add a service line and resell it across its book.** Nester's Hub sells paid, SEO, social, email, web and video — it has no ops/conversion layer. That is precisely the adjacent line they lack, and precisely what we'd be handing over.

**The same fact is the largest opportunity in this project.** Nester's Hub claims 100+ brands. ValenceOps has zero clients and needs Case Study #1. An agency running paid for clinics with no layer behind the click is a distribution channel — one relationship, many clinics. Tilak reached for the partnership angle already; he attached it to the freelancer branch. **It is materially stronger on the agency branch.**

### The frame that works on Gino specifically

Gino is judged on reach, CPL and creative. He is **not** judged on whether the front desk replies. But when Dr. Ruby asks *"we've spent all this, where are the patients?"* — that question lands on him, and **he cannot answer it with anything he controls.** He can show 91K reach. He cannot show bookings.

That gap is his personal exposure, and it is the only thing he actually needs from us.

**So: "we make your numbers provable" — never "your front desk is broken."** The second is an accusation about his client's staff delivered by a stranger; he will defend it, and defending it is the end of the meeting. The first compliments his work truthfully, makes us complementary rather than competitive, gives him something he wants for himself, and is true.

*(MEMORY §2: open with what's working, then frame the miss as a shared structural risk — never a verdict on their team.)*
