# Aurilueur Esthetic Clinic — Revenue, Service Mix and Economics Research

**Compiled 5 September 2026.** Inputs: their own website (37 service pages, own published price tables), their Google Business Profile (271 reviews, full corpus scraped), Meta Ad Library (live check), Instagram profile (live check), and the verbal + form answers from Dr. Shabeeb.

**Purpose:** establish the economics well enough to frame a proposal for the systems Adi has scoped. This is the evidence layer, not the proposal.

**Epistemic key:** `confirmed` = verified by us against a primary source, dated · `stated` = Shabeeb told us, treated as reliable per `CLAUDE.md` §2b · `inference` = our reasoning from evidence, labelled as such · `hypothesis` = a guess, must be asked not asserted.

**Rule 5 line.** Everything in §1–§4 is traceable to a dated primary source and may be said to them. §7 is marked NEVER-SAY. §8 is what we still do not know.

## 0 · Verdict

**Aurilueur is a body-contouring and medical-weight-loss business that sells ₹40,000–₹1,20,000 packages, wearing the website of a general skin clinic.** The skin and hair menu is broad and mostly low-ticket; the money is in two lines, and both are lines whose economics are decided *after* the quote is given, not before.

**Three findings that should shape the proposal:**

| # | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Their own site publishes full price tables.** We do not need competitor benchmarks to build the economics. Their abdomen package is ₹40,000–₹60,000, published, and three separate patients independently name ₹50,000 / ₹80,000 / ₹1,00,000 spends in reviews | `confirmed` |
| 2 | **7 of the 10 sub-5-star reviews since Jan 2024 trace to the quote-and-follow-up layer, not to the clinical work.** Vague offers, promises not honoured after payment, no follow-up on outcome, waiting time. Two trace to clinical/hygiene | `confirmed` |
| 3 | **Follow-up at Aurilueur is a function of which person owns it, not of a process.** The same review corpus contains a patient praising a named coordinator for "diligent" check-ins, a patient saying nobody followed up at all, and — five days ago — a patient calling the payment follow-up "constant, excessive... daunting, pushy" | `confirmed` |

Finding 3 is the thesis. It is structural, it is non-accusatory, it is written in their patients' own public words, and it is checkable by Shabeeb in ninety seconds.

## 3 · The evidence on the gap — in their patients' own public words

All quotes verbatim from Google reviews. All dated. All checkable.

### 3.1 · The three quotes that make the argument

> **31 Aug 2026 · 4★** — *"I am extremely happy with my treatment outcomes... However, my experience was significantly marred by the finance and accounts department. Their behavior was overly pedantic, rigid, and aggressive regarding payments. **Receiving constant, excessive follow-up messages felt daunting, pushy, and highly unprofessional.**"*

> **30 Jul 2025 · 2★** — *"Good Doctors and their services were nice at the initial treatment. Unfortunately treatment didn't work for me for the long run causing other health issues and also **they did not further follow up with me** and understand the cause."*

> **12 May 2024 · 5★** — *"Dr Shabeeb's team is also excellent — **Alia has been fantastic, diligent at following up and checking in**, and very responsive to feedback."*

Read together: the follow-up machinery at Aurilueur runs at maximum intensity on **collecting money already owed** and at zero on **the patient whose outcome is drifting**. When a specific person owns it, patients name that person in five-star reviews. When nobody does, the patient says nobody called.

**This is not a criticism of any individual. It is what happens when follow-up lives in people's memory rather than in a system** — the good ones over-deliver, the busy ones drop it, and nobody can see which is happening.

## 5 · The economics — a lever table, not a revenue claim

Every input is labelled. Substitute his numbers and the table re-runs.

### 5.1 · Volume

| Input | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries per day | 15–20 | `stated` |
| Working days per week | 6 (Tue–Sun) | `confirmed` |
| **Enquiries per month** | **390–520** | `inference` from the two above |
| Conversion | 30–40% | `stated` — **but see §8.1, we do not know what it converts *from* and *to*** |
| Post-quote show rate | ~90% | `stated` |
| Front-desk / ops staff | 3, one of whom is the sales manager | `stated` |

### 5.2 · The break-even sentence

At their own published prices, **one recovered abdomen package is ₹40,000–₹60,000. One recovered two-area programme is ₹40,000–₹70,000. One three-month GLP-1 programme is ₹84,000–₹1,35,000.**

Whatever our monthly fee lands at, it should be expressed against **one** of those, per month. Not a percentage uplift, not a projection. One case. They can check the number on their own website.

### 5.3 · The front-desk time argument

`inference` throughout — the two rates below are **our assumptions, and must be presented as ours** so he can correct them.

| Step | Per enquiry |
|---|---|
| First response | 1 message |
| Qualification exchange | 2 |
| Quote delivered | 1 |
| Follow-ups after silence (**their own form answer: 4 or more**) | 3–4 |
| Appointment confirmation + WhatsApp reminder | 2 |
| Neodove fields, typed by hand across the lead's life | — |
| **Conservative total** | **~8 touches** |

390–520 enquiries × 8 = **3,100–4,200 outbound messages a month**, plus every CRM field entered manually, across three people.

At 3 minutes per touch including the Neodove entry, that is **155–210 hours a month**. Spread across three people over ~26 working days: **roughly 2 to 2.7 hours per person per day spent typing messages and CRM fields.**

**Do not present this as a saving.** Present it as a reallocation, because that is what it is and it is the stronger argument:

> The messages that can be written in advance — the confirmation, the reminder, the "you have not booked your next drainage session," the payment nudge — are the ones currently consuming that time. The message that cannot be written in advance is the phone call to a patient who was quoted ₹60,000 last Tuesday and has gone quiet. **Right now the first kind is crowding out the second.** Their own reviews show it: the payment chasing runs hard enough that a patient complained about it publicly last week, while the outcome follow-up is the thing another patient said never came.

That is the answer to *"where can the front desk's time go for the highest return."* It is not "we save you three hours." It is **"we give you back the three hours and point them at the ₹60,000 quote nobody has called about."**

### 5.4 · The multiplier nobody has counted

Every body-contouring sale creates **6 to 10 scheduled visits** — the contouring sessions plus the bundled lymphatic drainage that their patients rate most highly.

That means each ₹40,000–₹1,20,000 sale generates 6–10 separate opportunities to no-show, 6–10 reminder obligations, and 6–10 slots that go empty if the patient cancels. At 3.6 sub-5-star reviews a year mentioning scheduling and abandoned packages, this is where "no-shows" — the thing he named as the main pain — most plausibly lives.

**It also reframes the no-show problem correctly:** a no-show at Aurilueur is usually not a lost consultation, it is a **missed session inside a package that has already been paid for**, which costs them a chair-hour and delays the result the patient will judge them on. That is a very different system requirement from chasing a lost lead, and it is worth getting right before Adi's scope is presented.

## 7 · NEVER-SAY — internal only

**Roughly 40% of their 271 reviews arrived in a 24-day window, 27 Jun – 20 Jul 2023.**

The window contains ~105–115 five-star reviews, many one-line, several **verbatim duplicates** (*"services are good and the treatment are effective"* appears three times; *"Loved Environment"* twice; a two-line stem-cell review twice), and one review that recommends a different clinic entirely (*"Go to a teaching hospital like SkinLab where money is not the only motivation..."*).

**Why it must never be said to them:** it is unprovable from outside, it is an accusation, and it would end the relationship. ## 8 · What we still do not know — and what each answer changes

Ordered by how much it moves the proposal.

### 8.1 · What does "30–40% conversion" convert *from*, and *to*? — **blocking**

At 390–520 enquiries a month, 35% converting to **paid treatment** at their published prices would put monthly treatment revenue in a range that would make Aurilueur one of the largest single-branch aesthetic clinics in Bangalore. Cross-checked against ~3.7 organic reviews a month and five doctors on a six-day week, that reading does not hold up.

**Almost certainly it means enquiry → consultation booked**, not enquiry → paid treatment. `inference`.

**Nothing numeric goes into a client document until he confirms which.** If it is enquiry→consultation, then the consultation→paid conversion is a second, unmeasured number, and *that* gap is very likely the real money — and the thing he cannot see because Neodove is typed by hand.

Also: he told us **35–40%** verbally and the figure has since been written as **30–40%**. Pick one, source it, and use it consistently.

### 8.2 · Where is the no-show? — **blocking**

He named no-shows as the main pain, and separately said ~90% of quoted patients show for treatment. Those reconcile only if the loss sits at **first consultation** — or, per §5.4, at **individual sessions inside an already-paid package.** Two completely different systems. Ask before scoping.

### 8.3 · Do the Meta ads land in Messenger / Instagram DM rather than WhatsApp?

See §4. One click on their live ad resolves it. If yes, there is a paid-traffic channel outside the sales manager's inbox, and it changes the integration surface and therefore the setup tier.

### 8.4 · Form question 3 was left blank

*"What happens when an enquiry comes in outside your working hours?"* — unanswered. Given 108 unstaffed hours a week and ads running through them, this is the highest-value question on the form and the only one he did not answer. Ask it directly, and ask it about **Monday**, which is the concrete version.

### 8.5 · Retention on the weight-loss programme

What share of Foundation and GLP-1 patients reach month 3? Month 6? §2.2 argues this is the largest untouched pool in the clinic. Without the number it stays a `hypothesis`.

### 8.6 · Is the WhatsApp line a shared WhatsApp Business inbox or a personal handset?

Decides the entire integration. Belongs in the SOW assumptions schedule, not the proposal body (`taste-n-judgement.md` §3).

### 8.7 · What does a converted case actually bill?

Their published figures are ranges. The realised average across a month is the number the break-even sentence should use.

### 8.8 · Does cosmetic gynecology sell?

Six service pages, zero mentions in 271 reviews. If it sells, it is a high-ticket line whose enquiries are the most privacy-sensitive in the clinic — which has real consequences for how an automated first response is written.

### The two conclusions that matter

**1. Aurilueur is not losing patients on price.** Two of their negative reviews use the word "expensive," and it would be easy to accept that at face value. The market data says the opposite: on body contouring, their headline line, they are at or below every Bangalore competitor who publishes a number, in one case by a factor of three. **Whatever is costing them conversions, it is not the price.** That is worth knowing before we frame anything, and it is worth him hearing, because it redirects attention to the layer we actually fix.

**2. They publish course totals in a market that refuses to.** No Bangalore competitor publishes a CoolSculpting package price. Derma Solutions says packages are "customized"; Body Shape says costs are "discussed upfront during your consultation"; Kaya's price table is entirely "Get Price" links. Aurilueur publishes full tables with session counts and EMI thresholds.

That cuts both ways, and the proposal should use both edges:

- **The asset:** a patient who has price-shopped Bangalore arrives at Aurilueur's enquiry already knowing the number and having accepted it. **Their inbound enquiry is warmer than a competitor's**, which makes the cost of a slow or missed reply higher for them than for the clinic down the road, not lower.
- **The exposure:** their price is the one that gets shopped against. They cannot defend on price, because it is public and it is already low. The only defensible ground is the experience between the enquiry and the chair, and their own reviews say that ground is currently held by individual people rather than by anything repeatable.

**That is the commercial argument for the whole engagement, and it comes entirely out of public, checkable data.**

### Benchmark gaps, stated honestly

Four things could not be sourced and must not be asserted: a competitor CoolSculpting **course total** (the market publishes per-session only), **HIFU body** priced separately from face, **lymphatic drainage as an aesthetic add-on** (only physiotherapy rates exist, ₹500–₹3,000, a different service), and **vaginoplasty from any Bangalore clinic** (aggregators only, and they contradict each other and themselves). Where a figure above is derived rather than sourced, it is marked in the benchmark file.
