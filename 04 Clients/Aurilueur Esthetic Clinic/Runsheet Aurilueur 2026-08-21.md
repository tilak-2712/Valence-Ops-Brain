## 2 · The frame — say this, roughly

> Thanks for the time. Let me say what this is and isn't, so you're not waiting for a pitch.
>
> I'm not going to sell you anything on this call.
>
> What I'd like to do is understand how an enquiry actually moves through your clinic — from someone
> messaging you, to them sitting in your chair, to what happens if they don't. I'll ask a fair
> number of questions, some quite specific.
>
> Then in a couple of days I'll send you a short document: what I heard, the three things that look
> like they're costing you the most, and which one I'd do first. You read it and decide whether it's
> worth going further. Does that work?

**Wait for the yes.**

---

## 3 · The journey question — then stop talking

> Walk me through how a patient goes from first hearing about your clinic to sitting in your chair.
> Wherever you want to start.

Do not interrupt. Note the gaps, come back later.
**Listen for:** which channel they name first · policy vs. reality · the jump ("...and then they come
in") with nothing in between · **the moment "we" quietly becomes "I."** That last one is the wedge.

---

## 4 · The nine questions — triggers and follow-ups

**Speed and coverage**

1. *If a patient messages your clinic WhatsApp right now — this minute — how long until an actual person replies? Not the auto-message. A person.*
   → trigger: "under five minutes" said confidently, **or** any pause / laugh / "it depends"
   → follow: *"And at nine on a Saturday night?"* then *"Who's holding the phone — personal number or a clinic device?"*

2. *When someone calls and nobody picks up — what happens to that call?*
   → trigger: "nothing" / "they call back if they want" / a log that only exists on one handset
   → follow: *"Roughly how many of those in a day?"* — **inability to answer is itself the finding.**

3. *Are there patients you handle personally — bigger cases, referrals from doctor friends?*
   → this is where owner-dependency surfaces without you accusing anyone of anything.

**Then, in whatever order the conversation allows — follow-up, data, no-shows, quotes, staff:**

4. *Someone asks the price and doesn't book. What happens next?*
5. *Who decides that a lead is dead?*
6. *If I asked how many enquiries you got last month, where would you look?*
7. *What's your no-show rate — and how do you know?*
8. *When someone doesn't turn up, who calls them, and how soon?*
9. *If your front-desk person left tomorrow, what walks out with them?*

**A question asked and not followed is a question wasted.**

## 5 · Live evidence (35–42)

> Would you mind sharing your screen for a minute and opening the clinic WhatsApp?

This is where diagnosis stops being arguable. You're looking for: unanswered threads, the last
outbound message in each, and how far back the oldest unreplied one goes. **Do not react to what you
see beyond "mm, okay."**

## 6 · The mirror (42–47)

Play back what you heard **in their words**. Solve nothing. This is the highest-trust moment
available and it is destroyed by one helpful suggestion.

## 7 · Close (47–50)

> A short document by [name the day], then twenty minutes for you to decide.

Date it out loud. Get agreement on the date, not the document.

## 8 · The three ways this call dies

1. **You solve something live.** Strongest failure mode. Feels like the call going well. Empties
   Stage 3 of any reason to exist and makes you every other vendor with the answer pre-loaded.
2. **You talk more than half the time.** Falsifier — the call failed regardless of how it felt.
3. **You cite a clinic fact you don't have.** You have none for this name. One invented specific and
   the room is gone.

---

## 9 · The one thing worth walking out with

**Who actually signs.** Multi-partner sign-off with no single decision-maker is a stated hard kill.
The cheapest place to learn it is this call. Ask it plainly near the end:

> If this turned into something you wanted to run, who else would be in that decision with you?
