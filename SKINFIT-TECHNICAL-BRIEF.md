## 2 · The marketing engine `confirmed`

Verified directly against the live ad account.

- Roughly 20 active paid ads, launched in waves over roughly two weeks, with new creatives still being added through the period checked.
- Every ad opens a chat-based conversation (Messenger). None link to the website.
- Some ads open an in-platform lead form.
- Tripwire-style first-visit offers at three price points.
- Organic content (Instagram reels) had meaningful reach in the weeks before the paid ads launched.

---

## 3 · Enquiry handling — what was directly tested `confirmed`

Two tests, two different channels:

1. **The clinic's listed number.** A person replied immediately, no automation involved. When asked to put the concern in writing, the reply was that typing it out was difficult, and the conversation moved to a phone call. The concern itself was never captured, and nothing was logged in the thread.
2. **One of the live paid ads**, messaged outside business hours on a weekend. The ad was live and taking clicks at the time. The message had not been answered by the next business day.

## 4 · What the client stated directly `confirmed`

In the client's own words, from the meeting:
- Replies go out late.
- Callbacks often land at a time that doesn't suit the person who enquired.
- The front desk isn't always able to give the right information at the point someone is deciding.
- There is no follow-up, nurturing, or no-show recovery system today.

From their own tracking, for one recent week: enquiries arrive daily; only a small number converted to a booked consultation across the whole week; one online consultation was handled.

---

## 5 · Commitments already communicated to the client, in writing

A document has already been sent to the client's marketing contact, written to be forwarded to the founder. It contains six specific statements:

1. Patient records stay in their existing system — the new layer works on incoming messages, not medical records.
2. Nothing automated ever gives medical advice; it captures the enquiry and routes it to a human.
3. Only people who contacted the clinic first are ever messaged, within what the platforms' own messaging-window and opt-in rules permit. No purchased lists.
4. Every message sent is logged and visible to the clinic.
5. Access is granted and can be withdrawn by the clinic at any time; accounts and numbers stay in the clinic's name.
6. No patient data is copied or stored anywhere outside the clinic's own systems.

Statement 3 assumes messaging access is via an officially approved method rather than an unofficial one.

## 6 · The volume pattern `hypothesis`

Based on the ad structure and typical per-conversation costs in this market, expected enquiry volume works out higher than what the client's own tracking shows for the same period.

Possible readings: actual spend is lower than the structure suggests, the client's tracking counts only qualified enquiries rather than every raw conversation, or a share of conversations — particularly from the in-platform lead forms — are arriving and going untracked anywhere. All three are consistent with the facts in §2–§4. None is confirmed over the others.

## 7 · Existing audit material

A detailed audit checklist exists internally, covering CRM/lead fields, walk-in flow, paid vs. organic follow-up, booking, quoting, reminders, no-shows, and rescheduling. It is written as a live interview script — questions meant to be worked through on a call or in person, with a real system open, several of them phrased as instructions to an interviewer ("ask for actual recent timestamps, not an estimate," "get the real wording, not a paraphrase").

It names a specific CRM/practice-management product throughout, used as though confirmed. That product name was heard once, in conversation, and has not been independently confirmed.

It also includes requests for admin-level system access, billing figures, staff contact details, and full historical data exports.
