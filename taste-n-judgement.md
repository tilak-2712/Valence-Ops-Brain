# taste-n-judgement.md

**What this file is.** A running record of how Tilak judges work — copy, design, thinking, tone — drawn from actual corrections he has given, not from guesses about preference. It exists so the same note never has to be given twice.

**What this file is not.** It does not repeat the operating rules in `CLAUDE.md` (push back before producing, verdict first, deliverables to files) or the outbound doctrine in `MEMORY.md` and `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md`. Those are *rules*. This is *taste* — the judgment applied when the rules run out.

**Maintenance — standing rule, set by Tilak 2026-08-18. Never needs prompting.**

This file is updated **the same session**, every time he does any of the following:

- Gives feedback of any kind on work produced
- Requests a change with conviction, or rejects something already delivered
- Pivots the thinking in a different direction
- Offers a unique perspective, a new way of thinking, or a way of connecting dots not used before
- Expresses taste, judgement or preference — how something should look, feel, sound or read
- Corrects a fact, a framing, or a premise

Record the correction **and the principle underneath it**, because he almost always gives both: the wrong thing, and why it should not have been there at all. Add a rejected/accepted pair to §8 wherever there is a concrete before and after. If a new entry contradicts an existing one, overwrite it and note what changed rather than leaving both to be picked up at random.

**The point of the file is that the same note never has to be given twice.** It only works if it is written to when the feedback arrives, not reconstructed afterwards.

*Seeded 2026-08-18 from the SkinFit Wellness engagement and a read of this folder.*

## 0 · The through-line

**He judges every artefact by simulating the person receiving it, never by whether it is clever.**

Every rejection in the record traces back to this. Copy is killed because *"im pretty sure they wont understand it either."* A line is killed because it is *"harsh."* A CTA is killed because it *"screams softness and no authority."* A number is killed because the reader can check it and find it wrong. He is not editing for craft. He is standing where the reader stands and reporting what he feels there.

**If a change cannot be justified in terms of what the reader will experience, it is decoration.**

## 1 · Copy and language

### Banned outright

| Pattern | Why it was killed | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Em dashes** | Overused, writerly. *(Exception: inside a verbatim client quote — their words stay untouched)* | Comma, colon, or a full stop |
| **Abstract or literary lines** | *"One half of the journey was designed. The other half grew"* and *"nothing is improvised"* — both rejected as opaque | The concrete version: *"The clinic has built a careful system for the patient in the chair. The same care has not yet been built for the patient trying to reach you."* |
| **Numbers derived from recollection** | *"quoting numbers that we remember very vaguely is not really a good sign"* | The qualitative version, or the unit mismatch: *"Enquiries arrive daily. Consultations are counted by the week."* |
| **Half-remembered proper nouns** | A software name he mentioned once ended up in four places, including a diagram caption. He wasn't sure it was even correct | *"your patient records"*, *"the diary"* |
| **Naming individuals unnecessarily** | *"we walk you and Dr. Ruby through"* → *"we walk you through"*. Also applies to our own side | Say the thing without the names |
| **Catchy headlines in a warm document** | *"you still have that one-pager doc vibe. stop with the catchy headlines"* | Plain descriptive headings |
| **Borrowed authority** | Industry stats and research citations to a 20-year marketer read as junior-vendor behaviour | Their own numbers, or a live demo they can test |

### Required

- **"Mr. <Name>"** in document copy, not first name alone. Indian business register.
- **Their words, verbatim, early.** Quoting the client back to themselves does more work than analysis. It also proves you were listening.
- **Every claim traceable** to something we tested or they said. Facts we verified ourselves (ad counts from the Ad Library, our own timestamped shop) may be stated precisely. Anything recalled second-hand may not.

## 2 · Tone and register

**Register must match the relationship stage.** This is the correction he named most explicitly. A cold diagnostic one-pager and a post-meeting proposal are different instruments, and reusing one as the other is a category error, not a style choice.

| Stage | Instrument | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | Diagnostic one-pager | A startling observed fact, earns a reply from a stranger |
| Reply received, meeting already booked (pre-meeting) | Cross-clinic pattern brief — category-level, not clinic-diagnosed | Proof of research depth and category fluency, explicitly deferring the specific diagnosis to the meeting |
| Post-positive-meeting | Review and proposal | Information, framing, and a clear next step. No hook needed — they already engaged |

**A pre-meeting document must not contain the specific fix.** *(Aurilueur, 2026-08-23.)* First draft for a clinic with a positive reply and an already-booked in-person meeting was a Stage-3-style findings-and-rollout document — clinic-specific diagnosis, ranked findings, a week-by-week plan. Rejected: *"if we give him all the systems before Tuesday's meet itself there won't be any reason for the meet."* Correct instinct — `SALES_MOTION.md` Rule 2 ("diagnosis and prescription never happen in the same conversation") was written for the live call and quietly violated by a document that front-loaded Stage 3 content before the Stage-2-equivalent conversation had happened. The fix that shipped: a cross-clinic pattern brief — five things observed across 100+ researched / a dozen-plus mystery-shopped Bangalore clinics, capability-level only ("what we build," no plan, no timeline, no data ask), explicitly naming that the specific diagnosis is what the in-person meeting is for. Proof of depth without pre-solving. **Generalizes:** any asset sent before a scheduled diagnostic conversation — not just what's said live on a call — can empty that conversation of its reason to exist if it's specific enough to *be* the diagnosis.

**Scope discipline: do not offer anything outside Bangalore, including free work.** *(2026-08-25.)* An offer to shop Maya's Hyderabad branch was cut as out of scope for the current stage. Applies even when the offer costs nothing and would strengthen the evidence: offering work in a city not being served implies a multi-city engagement that cannot be delivered, and a founder will read the offer as a claim about capacity. Keep every offer inside the market actually being worked.

**The strongest CTA is often a promise the last document already made.** The Maya one-pager stated in writing that it could not tell whether the silent Saturday was a one-off. Offering to go and settle that specific question outperformed every invented ask, because it is the document keeping its own word rather than a new thing being pitched. Before writing a follow-up CTA, re-read what the previous asset said it could not confirm.

**The best zero-cost ask is one whose answer you also want.** *(Chaitanya, 2026-08-25 — the demo was ruled out as the CTA.)* With the demo number off the table and a call already too heavy, the ask that worked was an offer to mystery-shop a second branch and report back. It costs the reader one word, needs no calendar, and gives a founder who is not in the country something she cannot otherwise get: an outside read on a site she is not standing in. It also produces the second instance `wedge-signal-entry.md` §0.2 requires before a multi-branch brand will treat a finding as a system rather than an incident. **Asking permission first also settles the open gotcha question** in §9 — an announced shop cannot read as a trap.

**Four things a follow-up must have, and the failure is usually all four at once.** *(Chaitanya, 2026-08-25.)* A revised follow-up was rejected on four counts in one line: *"thursday or friday is also not good. and also the message is vague, no clear articulation, no clear ask (cta). and no value-prop."*

- **Do not propose days to someone in another country who has not asked for a meeting.** The assertive two-named-slots rule was learned on warm, post-meeting documents and does not transfer to a stalled thread with a founder abroad. There, the right ask is the one needing no calendar at all: the live demo number.
- **Articulation means a thing that happened, not a concept.** *"How you'd know if it slipped again"* is an abstraction. *"You found out because a stranger messaged you about it"* is the same point as an event she experienced, and cannot be argued with.
- **Availability is not an ask.** *"Thursday or Friday works my end"* contains no verb directed at the reader. A CTA needs one action, stated imperatively.
- **Every message states what they get.** A problem plus a meeting request is not a value proposition. Name the capability in plain terms, and pick the part that matters to *this* reader: for a founder who is not in the country, the record she can check from anywhere outranks the speed.

**Don't let the strategic reasoning show inside the message.** *(SkinFit/Gino and RUA/Dr. Jatin, 2026-09-06 — three drafts across two clinics rejected in one line as "reads as a tactic, doesn't sound like me, wrong lever entirely.")* Drafts built correctly to the brief he'd given — a payoff stated for them, a different angle, a clear CTA — still failed because the reasoning behind them was written into the copy itself: *"one thing we could get moving without waiting on that,"* *"the operations one doesn't actually need you,"* *"not pushing, just want the timeline to stay real."* A recipient can feel managed the moment a message explains its own maneuver. The fix that held: state a fact that already happened — their own words, a date, a thing they did — then one direct, assertive ask, with every hedge phrase removed (*"no issue there," "totally understand," "not pushing," "just wanted to"*). This is the four-things rule below restated at the level of language rather than structure: all four boxes can be checked and it still reads as a tactic if the sentences soften around themselves or narrate their own cleverness.

**Two more tells, same session, immediate next round.** The "state a fact, then one assertive ask" fix above still landed as *"too pushy and salesy"* on SkinFit and *"doesn't seem to fit right"* on RUA. Two separate causes, not one:
1. **An "if X... otherwise Y" clause is an ultimatum, not a CTA**, no matter how reasonably it reads on its own. *"If not, let's lock Wednesday or Thursday"* and *"otherwise let's get both back to us by Monday"* both construct a deadline the recipient never asked for. Drop the "otherwise" branch entirely — state the ask once, plainly, with no fallback clause attached.
2. **A callback to a fact must match the register of the actual thread it's pulling from, not the register of a proposal.** Gino's own last line was casual and warm (*"sure, thanks for this, give me a days time will get back to u on this"*) — replying to it with a flat, businesslike statement of fact ("it's been a few days since you said...") ignores the tone he set and reads as a different, colder person than the one texting him. **"Doesn't pull from the last text" means the reply has to sound like the same conversation, not just reference the same event.** For a cold or never-warm thread like RUA, the opposite failure applies: reaching back to a much earlier positive signal (*"you forwarded this to your team"* from weeks before) to contrast it against current silence reads as an implied reproach — a tactic again, just a quieter one. Simplest fix in both directions: describe only what's still outstanding, skip the history lesson.

**Never route around the person you are writing to.** *(Chaitanya, 2026-08-25.)* A follow-up to a founder proposed taking the thread to her branch team and returning to her only if it proved worthwhile. Rejected: it *"bluntly asks who's responsible for B'lore and telling her to keep outside of the loop."* Two rules underneath. **Routing is the recipient's decision, never ours to assign** — a question like *"who runs Bangalore?"* implies she is the wrong person to be talking to, which is an odd thing to tell someone whose attention you are asking for. And **an offer to speak to someone else belongs last and conditional** (*"if X is a better place to start, happy to take it there instead"*), where it reads as flexibility, not redirection. Same event: a stalled-thread follow-up ran ~90 words. **On a thread that has already gone quiet, shorter is not politeness, it is the whole repair** — length reads as pressure.

**Never harsh about the prospect.** *"you've mentioned a branch that has no reputation yet. that's harsh."* Anything that could sting when read aloud in their own office gets softened. Frame gaps as structural, never as a verdict on their people. *"None of this replaces the front desk"* exists for exactly this reason.

**Assertive, not accommodating, and never dominating.** The clearest single instruction he has given on tone:

> *"the call-to-action is too vague and kinda screams softness and no authority... im not saying to make it extremely direct, harsh and dominating also. but be assertive, respectful of both of our times"*

**The shape that satisfied him:** give a reason the timing matters *to their business*, propose a window, then ask for a choice.
> *"We would suggest doing this in the coming week. The ads are already live and the reels are still gaining reach… Send us two days that work and we will confirm one."*

Rejected: *"Tell us a day that suits and we'll fit around the clinic's schedule."*

## 3 · Design judgment

- **He can tell repackaging from redesign.** *"visually, I don't think there's much change. You've added a diagram."* Adding elements to a borrowed chassis is not a new visual language. Changing the type scale, the palette, the structural devices and the page architecture is.
- **He checks execution, not just concept.** He caught a diagram clipped on the left edge that automated checks had missed because they only tested one side. **Verify all four edges, every time.**
- **He notices density.** *"too closely packed and might need slight rearrangement."* When something feels crowded, the fix is usually structural (move a label to the other side of the node) rather than shaving pixels.
- **Compression to hit a page count is a false economy.** Squeezing type to force 3 pages produced something that read as cramped. A cover plus generous pages beat a tight page count.
- **He responds to scale and restraint.** The dark full-bleed cover with the clinic's name at 64px was the single thing he called *"professional, premium and clean."* Big, bold, quiet.

**Standing format rules:** no *"Confidential — prepared for X"* footer, page numbers only. Clinic name large and bold; Valence Ops wordmark above it.

**The Enquiry Handling Review PDF is now the house chassis, not one document's styling.** *2026-08-22.* Asked for the proposal to be rebuilt so that *"the design theme, font, colour, cover page, font style, size everything has to be matched"* with the review already sent to Mr. Gino. The Word draft's own look was discarded without discussion. What this settles: a client who has read one Valence Ops document should recognise the next one as the same object before reading a word of it. The system, extracted from that PDF and now kept in `Proposals/_source/skinfit-proposal/`:

| | |
|---|---|
| Type | Poppins 400/500/600 for everything, Menlo for labels, numbers, footers and the kicker |
| Page | A4, 82px side margins, cream `#FBF9F5`, panels `#F3F0E9`, rules `#E4DFD6` |
| Ink | Headings `#14171A`, body `#3C4147`, muted mono `#7A7F84` |
| Green | `#0E7C5F` on light, `#4FBF9A` and `#8FCDB8` on dark |
| Dark | `#0B1F19` for the full-bleed cover and every callout box |
| Devices | Mono section numbers above a 21px heading; mono label column beside content; big mono card numerals; one dark callout carrying the ask |

**Commercials belong at the end of the proposal, not in a separate quote document.** Same date. The price is the last thing read before the next-step callout, after the systems, the delivery shape and the reasons. It also gets its own page with nothing else on it, which is the `#3` scale-and-restraint instinct applied to a number instead of a name.

**Multi-founder, multi-branch clinics get the generic-addressee treatment, not a guessed hierarchy.** *2026-08-26, RUA Skin & Hair Center — three co-equal founders, two branches.* Asked directly rather than left to inference: address one founder (the dossier's identified likely buyer) or all three or nobody by name. Chose nobody by name — which turned out to already be how the SkinFit document was built (no "Dear Mr. Gino" anywhere in the PDF body; the name lives only in the covering message). **The rule this confirms: individual names belong in the outreach message, never inside the document itself**, so the same proposal template survives a single-owner clinic and a three-founder one without editing its address logic.

**A fact you verified yourself belongs in the proposal; a fact you'd have to build to know belongs on the form.** Same session. RUA runs two branches on two separate, already-observed WhatsApp numbers — that's stated as fact in the proposal. *How* to wire them (one unified inbox vs. two routed ones, which platform each currently runs on) is a build decision nobody had asked about yet, so it went on the info-request form instead, alongside the still-open hard-kill checks (enquiry volume, treatment value). Same split as the SOW's own Assumptions & Re-Scope section — general/verified in the proposal, unresolved/technical on the form or in the SOW's assumptions list, never invented in either place.

## 4 · Evidence and epistemics

- **A vague referent in *their* message is as dangerous as one in ours.** The no-vague-referents rule has always been applied to outgoing copy. 2026-08-25 extended it to incoming: a clinic replying *"I'll see to it tomorrow"* names no object, and a plan built on the most convenient reading of "it" is a plan built on an assumption. Ask what the sentence would mean under each reading, and choose the move that works under all of them.
- **"More articulate than they could have put it themselves" is the bar for a helpful asset.** Tilak's framing for what to send a contact who has not asked for anything: not a pitch, not a follow-up, something that states the problem they may be having with more precision than they have available. The pressure it puts on the work is useful — if the document only restates what they already know, it is not a gift, it is a nudge wearing a gift's clothes.

He has an unusually strong nose for **unearned confidence**, and catches it in every register:

- A number multiplied out of an approximation
- A product name that was mentioned once in passing
- A fact about our own company that nobody verified
- An inflated self-rating *(he asked twice for an honest one and noticed when the first was generous)*
- Research citations standing in for evidence we don't have

**The test he applies:** can the reader check this, and what happens if they do? Israni has the real enquiry sheet, knows the real software, and sets the real ad budget. One wrong detail makes every other claim suspect.

**Corollary he stated himself:** don't design an ask the client may not be able to fulfil. He rejected *"thirty days of enquiries"* because they may not have that data. The fix was *"the past two weeks or more"* plus *"the message threads themselves are enough. There is no report to prepare and nothing to export."*

## 5 · Commercial instincts

- **Don't give away what could be sold.** He pulled the *"two things worth doing regardless of us"* section: *"we're considering setting that up for them or not… we should discuss it with them still."*
- **Don't promise capability we haven't confirmed.** Anything touching compliance or platform rules is flagged for Adi before it ships.
- **Sequence over speed.** On the agency partnership he asked directly whether he was making a bad call, took the re-sequencing (prove it on one clinic first), and did not push back. **He will change a plan when the reasoning is shown, not when the conclusion is asserted.**
- **Diagnosis before prescription, and he wants it framed as method.** The doctor analogy — *you don't recommend a treatment before the scan* — turns a no-cost audit into professional practice rather than a discount. He kept this untouched through every revision.
- **"At no cost", never "free".** *"don't use 'free' sounds cheap."*

- **A worked example under every question is how he lowers the cost of an honest answer.** In the SkinFit intake form he wrote a sample answer beneath each question, several of them modelling the negative: *"or, we don't really have one, it's just WhatsApp and a notebook."* The example does not just clarify the question, it gives permission to admit the gap, which is the answer that actually shapes a build.

- **His instinct on cost recovery is to fold the unallocated into the setup fee and price everything ongoing as a retainer.** *"the rest we'll collect as build/setup fee… every month there will be a retainer fee for ongoing maintenance & optimization."* The shape is sound. The correction offered: split vendor costs by whether they scale with the client's own volume, because the whole pitch is that enquiry volume rises, so any volume-linked cost sitting inside a flat retainer means a successful month costs him money. Fixed infrastructure absorbs into the fee; per-conversation and per-inference charges go on the client's card.
- **"Maintenance and optimization" is how a retainer dies.** Named as a framing risk, not a pricing one. By month four nothing is visibly breaking and the client starts asking what they are maintaining. The register that survives: he is not maintaining software, he is operating a service, and the retainer is named by what it delivers each month rather than by the category of work.

## 6 · How he works with me

- **Plan together before building.** *"let's discuss and sort what all things to be prepared for… and then you can go about preparing for that."* On strategic work, propose the shape and get agreement before producing the artefact.
- **He wants the method, not just the output.** He asked to be walked through exactly how a research finding was reached so he could do it himself next time. Explain the mechanism, including the luck and the dead ends.
- **Surgical scope.** *"rest everything, you keep as it is. no changes."* When he lists changes, change exactly those things.
- **He asks for brutal self-assessment and means it.** Rate honestly, break the rating into components, and say plainly when the thinking is better than the artefact.
- **He supplies real-world facts and expects them treated as reliable** — what a clinic replied, what an ad showed, what was said in a meeting. His *predictions and framings* are hypotheses to pressure-test. This distinction is already canon in `CLAUDE.md` §2b and it has held every time.

## 7 · How he thinks

- **He runs his own research thread in parallel** and arrives with findings (the Israni/Nester's Hub connection, the Messenger form behaviour, the branch being new). Assume he has looked.
- **He thinks in branches.** Before the SkinFit meeting he mapped who Gino might be, what each case would mean, and what to gatekeep in each. He wants to be *"prepared for all possible turn of events."*
- **He separates what to share from what to hold.** Gatekeeping is a first-class concern for him, not an afterthought.
- **He looks for the take-home regardless of outcome** — what we learn even if the deal dies.
- **His selling philosophy, in his words:** *"the actual sale lies in asking good questions and making themselves answer the question so that they sell themselves… while I am paving the conversation."* Build for questions that make the prospect state the problem, not for arguments that state it at them.

- **He re-sequences rather than deletes.** Told that the intake form was adding friction as a gate, he did not drop it. He moved it to run in parallel with the proposal. *"can i send over the form for information and while thats being done, ill send over the other details or systems and quotes."* Sequencing is his lever. Before recommending that something be cut, check whether it survives being moved.
- **He stress-tests a recommendation by restating it in its worst form.** *"so you want me to send a general proposal + quote for all services?"* He was not proposing that. He was checking whether my recommendation collapsed under the least charitable reading. State the scope precisely the first time, because an ambiguous recommendation will be handed back at its weakest.

- **He splits questions by type and hands back the ones that are not his.** Asked three questions at once, he answered the commercial one himself with conviction and returned both technical ones as *"what do you suggest is best?"* / *"what's best to do?"* Strategy, sequencing and commercial shape are his. Procedure, mechanism and best practice are mine to decide and defend. Offering him a menu on a technical question reads as ducking the call.
- **Controlled execution over aggressive rollout.** On going straight to a paid build: *"straight to paid build, but controlled execution. not instant rollout aggressively of all systems."* Delivery pace is a trust instrument for him, not just a project plan. One system proven before the next begins, because a first client watching a staged rollout is being shown competence, and a first client watching everything switch on at once is being asked to take it on faith.
- **"We don't know their tools yet" is a pricing objection he will raise, and the answer is to separate the two numbers.** The monthly fee prices running the service and does not move with their stack. The setup fee is where their stack actually bites. Answer the objection by naming which number carries the uncertainty, not by softening both.

**A client is sized by their integration surface, not by their prestige.** *(Aurilueur, 2026-09-05.)* He framed Aurilueur as *"not a normal high profile portfolio client"* — single branch, lower ad scale, no in-house brand or marketing staff, unlike SkinFit and Mr. Drish — and proposed treating it as proof-of-work with *"minimal systems with minimal ongoing maintenance."* The framing is right about the difference and points the wrong way about the consequence. Single branch, one CRM, one channel, one inbox owner is a **Tier A/B integration surface** against SkinFit's Tier C (Messenger-first, two branches, unknown PMS, attribution). That makes Aurilueur the cheaper build and the more likely one to *complete*, which is what a first case study actually needs. Two corrections that follow: **"minimal maintenance" fights the retainer's own justification** — `§5` already says *"maintenance and optimization" is how a retainer dies*, because the fee prices **operating** a service, and a build-and-hand-over job is an ₹8,000–₹18,000/month market, not ₹40,000. If minimal is right, it must mean **fewer systems live at once**, not less operating, which is also his own *"controlled execution, not aggressive rollout."* And **do not discount the monthly to buy a case study** — `PRICING-BUILD-COST` §4.2 settles it: waive the one-time, never the recurring, because ₹40,000 is the number every future client anchors to and these owners talk.

**Research the client's own published prices before reaching for competitors.** *(Aurilueur, 2026-09-05.)* Asked for ticket sizes with competitor scraping as the fallback if the clinic published nothing, the fallback turned out to be unnecessary: their own site carried full price tables for nine treatment lines, with session counts and EMI thresholds. Three patients independently named their actual spends in public reviews, and those matched the published tables. **A number from the client's own website is the strongest evidence class available** — it passes the `§4` test completely (they can check it, and checking it confirms us), it needs no hedging, and it cannot be argued with. Competitor benchmarks then do a different and smaller job: not sizing the ticket, but establishing **where they sit**, which produced the finding that mattered. Reach for the target's own published material first, every time.

**Code the negative reviews by root cause, not by sentiment.** *(Aurilueur, 2026-09-05.)* Reading 271 reviews and counting stars produces nothing usable. Sorting the ten sub-5-star reviews since a fixed date into *quote-stage expectation · follow-up absence · scheduling · clinical* produced the line the whole proposal rests on: seven of ten sit between the enquiry and the chair, two are clinical and not our business. The same pass surfaced the three quotes that prove follow-up is **person-dependent rather than absent** — one patient praising a named coordinator's diligence, one saying nobody ever called, and one, five days old, calling the payment chasing *"constant, excessive... daunting, pushy."* That triangle is a structural finding stated entirely in the client's patients' words, which is the only way to name a failure without `§2`'s harshness. Extends the standing rule *(read review text, not just ratings)*: **read it, then classify it against the thing you sell.**

**"Not losing on price" is worth proving before framing anything.** Same session. Two of Aurilueur's negative reviews use the word "expensive," which invites accepting price as the problem. The benchmark data said the opposite: on body contouring their whole 2–3 session package costs what one Bangalore competitor charges for a single session. **A complaint in a review is a datapoint about perception, not about the market.** Check it before letting it shape the diagnosis, because a wrong problem framed confidently is worse than no framing.

**A scope built from a self-report intake will automate what the clinic already does well.** *(Aurilueur, 2026-09-05.)* Adi's three proposed systems — speed-to-lead, appointment reminders, no-show recovery — map one-to-one onto the three intake answers where Shabeeb described an existing process ("someone calls right away", "Whatsapp reminder", fast first response). The four things excluded from scope (quote follow-up, slot fill, nurture past silence, reschedule) map onto the evidence of where the money actually leaks. **Multiple-choice self-reports flatter the respondent; a checkbox saying "someone calls right away" reads as "a process exists" when the review corpus says it is person-dependent.** Rule: build scope from the evidence layer, then use the form to *confirm or contradict* it — never the other way round. The rebuttal test (`wedge-signal-entry.md` §0.1) applies to a proposal exactly as it applies to a cold DM.

**When a clinic is fast because three people are doing it by hand, we are not selling speed.** *(Tilak, 2026-09-05.)* His observation that Aurilueur's response times are already good and that they are *"overstaffed... hired more for the role than it actually needs"* reframes the entire sale. The pitch is not "you are losing leads", which is rebuttable, it is **the same output at lower labour, plus headroom to grow ad spend without a fourth hire.** Two things follow. **Never say "overstaffed"** — it implies firing people, it is harsh per `§2`, and it is not our call; the frame is **absorption**, i.e. who handles the next 200 enquiries a month when the ads scale. And the cost of not fixing becomes **the next headcount**, which is his own arithmetic and cheaper for us to argue than any projected uplift.

**Pipes are not content, and a scope that omits the message is half a scope.** Same session. His note that their information is *"very generic, not much conversion-focused"* names a gap no automation system closes by itself: the proposed systems all send messages, and nothing in the scope says what the messages say. Their own lead-capture page leads with a ₹2,499 offer and a 50%-off voucher into a clinic whose real sale is a ₹40,000–₹60,000 package, which manufactures the expectation mismatch that five of their ten negative reviews describe. **Message content belongs in the scope as a named deliverable**, or we build pipes and the same generic water goes through them faster.

**The machine decides *when* to call and catches what the call misses. It never replaces the call.** *(Tilak, 2026-09-05.)* His correction: *"a call is comparatively very effective over text unless they're finding the patient at a wrong time."* Both halves are evidenced in Aurilueur's own reviews — a patient crediting conversion to a phone call (*"Rani had explained about the service in brief over the call which made me come down"*), and a patient publicly complaining about *"constant, excessive follow-up messages."* **Call converts; unsupervised text harasses.** The design that follows is human-first: the system prepares the call list with context, the human calls, one-tap outcome, and **text fires only on "no answer", at a different time of day than the call** — because no-answer is usually a timing failure and repeating the window repeats the failure. **The most valuable feature is knowing when to stop**, which is precisely what is missing today and what produced the public complaint.

**Do not automate a decision the clinic itself makes by improvisation.** *(Tilak, 2026-09-05.)* On scheduling and rebooking he argued against automation because doctor availability is unstructured, lives in the sales rep's head, and one walk-in treatment reorders the day. Correct, and it generalises into the scope principle: **the system never makes a calendar or clinical decision, it removes the typing and the remembering.** The bot books a consultation *request*, not a confirmed slot; the rep confirms against the real diary. When the day collapses, the rep taps the affected names and the system sends the apologies, collects the replies, and hands back a list — **rep decides, system types.** A build that tries to own a calendar it cannot see will be wrong within a week and blamed for it, and it is also the expensive end of the integration surface in `PRICING-BUILD-COST` §1, so declining it protects the setup fee. The elegant part: this design **needs** the chaos to be valuable, which converts his objection into the argument.

**Automate the labour, never the judgment — structure is a precondition for automating a *decision*, not for automating work.** *(Tilak, 2026-09-05, raised as a possible bias of his own.)* His held belief was *"a process has to be fully automated if the process is clear and structured each time"*, alongside the sharp commercial instinct that *"general automation has no scope now and any ₹5k/mo SaaS can do that and we'll be compared to that."* **Keep the second half, invert the first.** A clear, structured process is precisely the process that does not need us — a BSP at ₹1,500–₹3,000/month plus a template flow does it, and we become a markup (`PRICING-BUILD-COST` §3). The value sits in the messy processes where the *decision* needs a human but the *labour* does not. Aurilueur's decisions are unstructured (doctor availability, what to quote, which patient to push); the labour around them — typing, remembering, timing, logging, chasing — is highly structured even when the decision is not. **That seam is the product**, and it dissolves the false choice between "automate it fully" and "leave it manual."

**Channel sprawl is the finding, not the obstacle.** Same session. He counted the inbound doors and worried the complexity was unnavigable: three phone numbers, two emails, two domains, WhatsApp *"managed by sales manager"* against three front-desk staff, plus Messenger, Instagram DM, GBP calls and multiple forms. That fragmentation **explains three things at once** — why a self-reported "15–20 enquiries a day" is a count of what someone remembered to log rather than what arrived, why three people feel fully occupied at that volume (the cost is context-switching, not conversation count), and what the first system actually is: **not "24/7 WhatsApp" but one inbox behind every door**, one thread per patient regardless of arrival point. A ₹5k SaaS handles one clean channel. Nobody sells into the messy multi-surface reality, which is the differentiation he was looking for.

**The four rules that make a system survive human override.** Same session, answering *"can it be overridden by a human without the system failing or breaking."* ① **Takeover is a first-class state, not an exception** — a staff reply in a thread pauses automation on that thread, resuming only on completion or a set silence; staff learn in week one that touching a thread stops the robot, and that is what earns their trust. ② **Default to preparing, not sending** — deterministic messages (acknowledgement, reminder, confirmation) send; consequential ones (quote chase, reschedule, waitlist) queue for a human to release. The test is whether a wrong send would embarrass the clinic. ③ **Nothing is stored only in our system** — everything writes to their CRM, so if we vanish they keep the record, which is the direct answer to the real objection named in `SALES_MOTION.md` §1: exposure. ④ **Degrade to a notification, never to silence** — an integration that drops tells the rep "3 enquiries I could not process." The failure mode is a human being told, never a lead disappearing.

**Discount the scope, never the rate.** *(Aurilueur, 2026-09-05.)* Field fact from Tilak: Shabeeb called Neodove *"very cheap"* approvingly during the meeting, which puts his software reference frame around ₹3,000/month and makes a ₹40,000 retainer read as roughly 13× his anchor. The instinct to lower the number is right about the problem and wrong about the lever. Cutting the rate contradicts `PRICING-BUILD-COST` §4.2 and undermines the ₹40,000 already in writing to SkinFit, in a market where these owners talk. **Cutting the scope does not** — fewer systems for a smaller monthly is honest, whereas the same systems for a smaller monthly is a discount that travels to every future client. Sell Phase 1 alone at a lower monthly, publish the ladder for the later phases in the proposal itself so the rise is a stated term rather than a later surprise, and the ₹40,000 survives as the full-stack price. **The commercial shape and his own "controlled execution, not aggressive rollout" preference turn out to be the same instruction.**

**Anchor against the ad budget, not the software budget.** Same event. A clinic that celebrates a cheap CRM is not anti-spend, it is anti-overpaying-for-tools — and it chose a *telecalling* CRM, which is consistent with everything else observed there (the coordinator calls, and the call is what converts). The mistake is letting the comparison sit on the software line. **Ads are the cost of getting the enquiry; we are the cost of not wasting it** — same budget, same P&L logic, and against a real Bangalore ad spend the retainer is a fraction rather than a multiple. The second available anchor is a front-desk salary, since the argument is that they otherwise need a fourth. Never let the number be compared to a tool, because as a tool it is indefensible and as a function it is cheap.

**Sequence the document as problem, gap, bridge. The gap needs its own page.** *(Aurilueur proposal, 2026-09-05.)* The first draft went straight from *how enquiries are handled today* to *what we would build*, which reads as a vendor arriving with an answer. The fix that landed was a page in between: every step needs someone free at the moment it needs doing, that holds on a normal day and slips on a full one, the work is growing because the advertising is growing, so **at double the volume the only options are a fourth person or taking the repeatable work off the three you have.** The reader reaches the conclusion before the systems are named, which is what self-persuasion actually means. It also converts the cost of inaction into a headcount, which is his own arithmetic rather than a projection of ours.

**Never state a capability as a permanent refusal when it is really a setting.** Same session, Pratham's correction. The draft said the assistant *"does not quote prices"* flatly. It became *"it does not share prices. If you later want it to share standard package prices, that is switched on only when you ask for it."* A flat never reads as a limitation of the product; an approval-gated capability reads as control handed to the client. Same behaviour on day one, opposite impression.

**Two accuracy rules that keep costing us.** Same session. **Do not describe a capability against infrastructure nobody has confirmed** — the draft said the system takes forms from *"both websites"* when only one was ever discussed, which is the same failure class as a half-remembered product name. And **use the client's own vocabulary for their own operations**: *"the doctors' diary"* became *"availability"*, because the first is our word for their thing. Both are `§4` in a new costume: can the reader check this, and what happens when they do.
