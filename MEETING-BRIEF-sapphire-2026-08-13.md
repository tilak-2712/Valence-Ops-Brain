## C · Rapport — first 5 minutes

- **Open by closing the last loop yourself.** *"Last time you asked for a company profile and I sent a document without offering you a time. That was my mistake — that's why we're doing this properly."* Naming your own miss is the fastest trust move available and costs nothing.

- Keep the frame explicit: *"I'm not selling you anything on this call. I want to understand how an enquiry moves through the clinic. Then I'll send you a short document and you decide if it's worth going further."* **Wait for the yes.**

## D · Trust — the credential question (they WILL ask again)

Have these scripted. Do not improvise them.

- **"Who else do you work with?"** → *"Nobody in dermatology yet — you'd be the first clinic. I'd rather tell you that than invent a client list you'd find out about later."* Then immediately: *"What we do have is the method, and I'll show you exactly how it runs before any money changes hands."*
- **Turn early-stage into the asymmetry:** *"Client one gets two founders who cannot afford to fail. Client forty at an agency gets a junior account manager."* Say it once, plainly. Do not defend it twice.
- **Method transparency over claims** — this is the only honest differentiator you own. Show *how* the work gets done, on their clinic, before invoicing.
- **Be explicit about what you could NOT verify** from outside. Naming your own blind spots does more for credibility than any claim.
- **Offer something testable.** If the demo WhatsApp number is live, hand them the phone: *"Message it yourself, right now."* A claim they can test is not a claim.
- **De-risk exposure, not price.** The real objection in this market is *"will I still be paying for something dead in eight months."* Answer it before it's asked: free pilot, a named stop-clause, a refundable token deposit, an end-of-pilot review call booked before launch.
- If asked how you found what you found: answer honestly and briefly. Mystery shop, ad library, review reading, public data. **The honesty is the product demo.**

## E · Problem discovery — the Sapphire-specific question bank

### The three hypotheses to test (do not lead with them, test them)

| # | Hypothesis | Evidence you already have |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No single enquiry funnel** — 4 published phone numbers across 2 branches | 080 2654 9666 · 97417 83333 · 080 2963 6666 · 70707 03152. Two of these also sit in the Instagram bio. |
| 2 | **Multi-session drop-off is the biggest silent leak** | PRP, laser, chemical peels, Hydrafacial *packages*, acne scar courses — all multi-visit. Reviews prove they call after laser but only for some patients. |
| 3 | **Paid spend with no click-to-booked visibility** | ~7 active Google Ads across acne scars, hair & nail, preventative derm, cosmetic. No Meta ads seen. |

### Open with one question, then stop talking

- *"Walk me through how a patient goes from first hearing about Sapphire to sitting in the chair. Start wherever you want."* **Do not interrupt.** Note where they get vague — that's the gap. Note where "we" quietly becomes "I."

### Speed and coverage

- *"You publish four numbers. If a patient messages the WhatsApp right now, how long before an actual person — not an auto-message — replies?"*
  → follow-up: *"And at 8pm on a Saturday, when you're open till 8:30?"*
- *"Who physically holds that phone — is it a clinic device or someone's personal number?"*
- *"When the desk doesn't pick up, what happens to that call?"* → *"Roughly how many of those in a day?"* **Inability to answer is itself the finding — note it, don't correct it.**
- *"An enquiry lands for a treatment that only runs at the other branch — what happens to it?"* (Cross-branch handoff is uniquely theirs.)
- *"Google shows a 'Book online' option on your profile — where do those bookings land, and who sees them?"* Most clinics don't know this channel is live.

### Follow-up reality

- *"Last twenty people who enquired and didn't come in — how many times did someone try to reach each of them?"*
  → follow-up, said lightly: *"If I asked your coordinator the same question, would I get the same number?"* **The hesitation is worth more than the answer.**
- *"Where does the coordinator track who still needs following up with?"* → *"If she were off sick for a week, who'd know which patients were mid-conversation?"* **Say this gently — it must not sound like criticism of her.**
- *"Is that the same person at both branches, or two people?"*

### The multi-session thread — Sapphire's strongest angle

- *"Your reviews mention staff calling patients after laser sessions to check on recovery. Who decides which patients get that call?"* **This is the whole call in one question.** They already believe in follow-up — you are asking whether it survives volume.
- *"Someone books a six-session package and stops turning up after two. Who notices, and when?"*
- *"How would you find, today, everyone who's mid-course and hasn't been in for two months?"*
- *"How many patients from a year ago have never come back? Can you get that number?"*

### Bookings, quotes, high-ticket

- *"Out of ten consults booked, how many don't turn up?"* → *"And when someone doesn't — what's the first thing that happens, and how soon?"*
- *"Someone gets a quote for a thread lift or hair restoration and says they'll think about it. What happens over the next two weeks?"*
  → *"Roughly how many quotes went out last month, and how many got a follow-up in the first two days?"* **The gap between those two numbers is usually the single biggest thing in the clinic — and they've never put them side by side.**

### Data and adoption

- *"Could you tell me right now how many enquiries you got last month across everything — WhatsApp, the four numbers, Instagram, walk-ins, Google?"* → *"Which of those are you least sure about?"* Their answer names their own blind spot better than you could.
- *"You're running ads on acne scars, hair, preventative derm. Do you know which of those actually converts into a booked consult?"*
- *"If something started messaging patients automatically — what would worry you about that?"* → *"What would it need to do for you to be comfortable?"* **Write this verbatim.** It goes in the findings document nearly word-for-word, and it is the adoption risk you must design around.

### Live evidence (ask even though it's awkward)

- *"Would you mind opening the clinic WhatsApp for a minute? Not looking at anything personal — just the shape of the last twenty conversations."*
- Look for: threads ending with a **patient's** message and no reply · gaps between message and answer · the same three sentences typed by hand · anything past the booking confirmation.
- **Say what you see. Interpret nothing.** *"That one's been sitting since the eleventh. Okay — useful, I'll come back to it."*
- If they won't share: *"Scroll back twenty and tell me how many end with a patient's message that never got answered."* Them counting is stronger than you counting.

### The mirror — before you explain anything

- Play back 4–5 things **in their own words**, solve nothing, then **stop talking and let the silence sit.** Founders add the most important thing in the call in that gap.

## F · Explanation — what to actually say about ValenceOps

The last asset failed because after reading it they still didn't know what you *do*. Fix that tomorrow — but late in the call, and short.

- **One sentence, no jargon:** *"We're not marketing. We don't bring you more enquiries. We fix what happens to the ones you're already paying for — from the moment someone messages to the moment they're in the chair, and after."*
- **Anchor to their own money:** they are already spending on ~7 Google Ads. Every enquiry that leaks is spend already paid for. **Cheaper than buying more clicks.**
- **Name the five mechanisms plainly, no product names:** instant enquiry capture and qualification on WhatsApp · consultation reminders · no-show recovery · quote follow-up on high-ticket · mid-course and dormant patient reactivation.
- **Frame automation as extending their own standard, not replacing staff:** *"You already call patients after laser. We're making sure that reaches everyone, not just the ones someone remembers."* This kills the "cold and robotic" objection before it forms.
- **Say what you are NOT:** not a call centre, not an agency, not more ad spend, not replacing their coordinator.
- **Two branches = the multiplier.** Whatever is designed once runs at both. Say it once.
- **Do NOT design the solution live.** When they ask *"so what would you do?"* — and they will:
  > *"I've got a fairly clear idea, but I'd rather not answer off the top of my head. Half of what you've told me I want to sit with. That's what the document is for — Thursday."*
  A small refusal here beats an answer. Everyone else answers immediately.
- If they push hard, give **the ranked three things only** — no rollout, no timeline.

## I · Landmines — read this twice

1. **Do NOT open with the mystery shop as an accusation.** The WhatsApp test was sent **Sunday after hours** — Sapphire's published Sunday hours are 10:30 AM–5 PM, and they replied **Monday 11 AM, which is opening time.** By your own Aug-11 admissibility rule that finding is **inadmissible and rebuttable.** If they push back you lose the room. Use it only if *they* raise after-hours handling.
2. **Their reviews are excellent — 4.7 across 724, almost no complaint pattern.** Any "your patients are unhappy" framing is factually wrong and they will know it. The gap here is **operational leakage at scale**, not service quality.
3. **Don't solve anything live.** It feels like the call going well; it empties the follow-up document of any reason to exist and makes you every other vendor.
4. **Don't talk more than half the time.** Target ~43%. If you talked more, the call underperformed regardless of how it felt.
5. **Don't repeat the deck.** They already have it and it didn't move them.
6. **Don't criticise the coordinator or the front desk.** Diagnose systems, never people.
7. **Don't leave without a date.** A warm reply with no booked time is worth approximately nothing — Sapphire already proved that once.
