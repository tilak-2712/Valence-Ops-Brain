# 03 — The Operations Call

**Owns:** Stage 2. 50 minutes, live, within 5 days of the reply.

**The one instruction that matters more than everything else in this file: you are not solving anything on this call.** The urge to prove competence by fixing something live is the strongest failure mode here. It feels like the call going well. It empties Stage 3 of any reason to exist, and it turns you into every other vendor who showed up with the answer already loaded.

**Relationship to `08 Collateral/Audit Call Docs/03 Discovery Call Question Map.md`:** that document is the full forensic instrument — owner and coordinator interviewed separately, five blocks, cross-checks against exported data. It is a *deployment* tool, run after a yes, with data in hand. This file is the compressed first-call version: one person, no data yet, 50 minutes. Everything here traces back to a question in that document. Don't run that one on a first call — you'll spend 50 minutes on Block A alone and the owner will feel interrogated.

---

## Before the call

**Confirm they watched the video.** A one-line WhatsApp the morning of: *"Looking forward to 4pm. If you get a spare six minutes, the walkthrough I sent means we can skip straight to your side of things."* If they haven't watched it, adjust — spend three minutes, not fifteen, and offer to send it again after.

**Re-read their dossier in `Pre-outbound-research/`.** Specifically: the mystery-shop timestamps, the ad activity, the review themes. You should be able to name their treatments and their branches without checking.

**Write your three hypotheses down before the call, and commit to being wrong.** From `wedge-signal-entry.md` §3, pick the three most likely wedges. Then run the call as though you have none, because the point of the call is finding out which — if any — is real. A hypothesis you're attached to turns questions into leading questions and the answers become worthless.

**Roles — both of you on, both with a job.**
- **Lead (the POC on the Notion row):** asks everything, manages the clock, does the mirror.
- **Second:** silent through the middle, taking notes verbatim where possible. Asks exactly one question at minute 42 — the sharpest thing that came up and wasn't followed. Then confirms the next step and date.

A second person with no job is worse than being alone — it reads as an audience. If the second can't attend, the call still runs; the lead just records notes immediately after, not later.

**Recording:** ask. *"Do you mind if I record this? Only so I'm not typing while you're talking — it doesn't go anywhere."* If they hesitate at all, drop it immediately and take notes.

## The shape

| Min | Block | You're doing |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Frame | Killing the expectation of a pitch |
| 3–8 | The journey question | One open question, then shutting up |
| 8–35 | Forensic blocks | 9 questions, following the triggers |
| 35–42 | Live evidence | Getting the inbox on screen |
| 42–47 | The mirror | Playing it back, solving nothing |
| 47–50 | Next step, dated | Booking the decision call |

**Targets:** talk about 43% of the time. Ask 11–14 real questions. Name zero solutions before minute 42.

---

## [0:00–3:00] The frame

Purpose, plan, outcome — stated out loud, in three sentences. It's worth the ninety seconds because it removes the thing they're braced for.

> Thanks for the time. Let me say what this is and isn't, so you're not waiting for a pitch.
>
> I'm not going to try to sell you anything on this call. I sent the walkthrough exactly so we don't have to spend this half hour on what we do.
>
> What I'd like to do instead is understand how an enquiry actually moves through your clinic — from someone messaging you, to them sitting in your chair, to what happens if they don't. I'll ask a fair number of questions, some of them quite specific.
>
> Then in a couple of days I'll send you a short document: what I heard, the three things that look like they're costing you the most, and which one I'd do first. You read it, and you decide whether it's worth going further. Does that work?

**Wait for the yes.** It's a small commitment and it makes the rest of the call collaborative rather than extractive.

---

## [3:00–8:00] The journey question

One question. Then stop talking.

> Walk me through how a patient goes from first hearing about your clinic to sitting in your chair. Wherever you want to start.

**Do not interrupt.** Not to clarify, not to sympathise, not to say "that's interesting." Note every gap you hear and come back to it later. Where they *start* the story tells you what they think their business is; where they get vague tells you where the system isn't.

Only prompt if they genuinely stall: *"And once someone's messaged — then what?"*

**Listen for:** which channel they mention first. Whether they describe a policy or a reality. Any point where the story jumps ("...and then they come in") with nothing in between. Whether the word "we" quietly becomes "I."

---

## [8:00–35:00] The forensic blocks — 9 questions

Nine questions, roughly three minutes each. Each maps to a block in `Audit_call_docs/03`. Ask them conversationally, in whatever order the conversation allows — this is a map, not a script to read aloud.

Each has a **trigger** (the answer that means something) and a **follow-up** (what to ask when triggered). The follow-up is where the value is. A question asked and not followed is a question wasted.

### Speed and coverage

**Q1.** *If a patient messages your clinic WhatsApp right now — this minute — how long until an actual person replies? Not the automatic message. A person.*
→ **Trigger:** "under five minutes," said confidently. Or any pause, laugh, or "it depends."
→ **Follow-up:** *"And at nine on a Saturday night?"* Then: *"Who's holding the phone when that happens — is it on someone's personal number, or a clinic device?"*

**Q2.** *When someone calls the clinic and nobody picks up — what happens to that call?*
→ **Trigger:** "nothing," "they call back if they want," or a missed-call log that only exists on the device.
→ **Follow-up:** *"Roughly how many of those in a day, would you guess?"* Note whether they can answer at all. Inability to answer is itself the finding.

**Q3.** *Are there patients you handle personally — bigger cases, referrals from doctor friends, anything sensitive?*
→ **Trigger:** any yes. Especially "I prefer to handle those myself."
→ **Follow-up:** *"And those conversations — do they live anywhere other than your phone?"* This is usually the highest-value segment in the clinic and the least visible one.

### Follow-up reality

**Q4.** *Think of the last twenty people who enquired and didn't come in. How many times did someone try to reach each of them?*
→ **Trigger:** any number given as a policy ("we try three times"). Any "it depends."
→ **Follow-up:** *"If I asked your coordinator the same question, would I get the same number?"* Ask it lightly. This is the narrative-divergence test compressed into one question, and the answer — or the hesitation — is often the most useful thing in the call.

**Q5.** *Where does your coordinator keep track of who still needs following up with?*
→ **Trigger:** anything that isn't a system. A notebook, a WhatsApp message to themselves, a separate sheet, "she just remembers."
→ **Follow-up:** *"If she were off sick for a week, who'd know which patients were mid-conversation?"* Ask this gently — it's not a criticism of her and it must not sound like one.

### Bookings and quotes

**Q6.** *Out of ten consults booked, how many don't turn up?*
→ **Trigger:** any number above 3. Also any inability to give a number.
→ **Follow-up:** *"And when someone doesn't turn up — what's the first thing that happens?"* Listen for "nothing" or "we move on." Then: *"How soon after?"*

**Q7.** *Someone comes in, gets a quote for a package, says they'll think about it. What happens over the next two weeks?*
→ **Trigger:** "they come back if they decide." Any absence of structure.
→ **Follow-up:** *"In the last month, roughly how many quotes went out? And of those, how many got a follow-up in the first two days?"* The gap between those two numbers is usually the single biggest thing in the clinic — and it's a number they've never put together.

### Data and adoption

**Q8.** *If I asked how many enquiries you got last month across everything — WhatsApp, calls, Instagram, walk-ins — could you tell me right now?*
→ **Trigger:** a round number, a pause, "I'd have to check," "depends how you count."
→ **Follow-up:** *"Which of those channels do you think you're least sure about?"* Their answer names their own blind spot better than you could.

**Q9.** *If something started messaging patients automatically — what would worry you about that?*
→ **Trigger:** any concern. Every owner has one, and the specific one they name is the adoption risk you'll have to design around.
→ **Follow-up:** *"What would it need to do for you to be comfortable with it?"* Write the answer down verbatim. It goes in the findings document nearly word-for-word, and it's the thing that makes them feel heard rather than sold to.

**If a question is already answered by the journey story, skip it.** Re-asking something they've told you is the fastest way to look like you weren't listening.

## [35:00–42:00] Live evidence

This is where diagnosis stops being arguable, and it's the block most likely to get skipped for being awkward. Ask anyway.

> Would you mind sharing your screen for a minute and opening the clinic WhatsApp? I'm not looking at anything personal — I just want to see the shape of the last twenty or so conversations.

**What to look at, out loud, without judgment:**
- How many threads end with the clinic's message and no reply
- How many end with the *patient's* message and no reply
- Gaps between a patient message and the clinic's answer — read a couple of timestamps aloud, neutrally
- How many are the same three sentences typed out by hand
- Whether anything exists past the booking confirmation

**Say what you see. Interpret nothing.**
> That one's been sitting since the eleventh. And that one. Okay — that's useful, I'll come back to this.

**If they won't share the screen** — and some won't, reasonably — don't push. Ask instead: *"Scroll back twenty conversations and tell me how many of them end with a patient's message that never got answered."* They'll usually tell you, and them counting it themselves is more powerful than you counting it.

**In-person variant:** if you're at the clinic, ask to stand at reception for ten minutes during a busy stretch. Watch which device the coordinator reaches for. `Audit_call_docs/01` §3.1 — this single observation confirms or kills the personal-phone silo faster than any question.

---

## [42:00–47:00] The mirror

The highest-trust moment available in the whole motion. Play back what you heard, in their words, and solve nothing.

> Let me say back what I think I heard, and tell me where I've got it wrong.
>
> You said a patient messaging at nine at night gets a reply the next morning, and you're not certain how often that's happening because it isn't recorded anywhere.
>
> You said your coordinator tries a couple of times and then marks it closed, and that where she keeps that is her own notebook rather than anywhere you can see.
>
> You said maybe three or four out of ten don't turn up for a consult, and that after that, nothing happens.
>
> And you said the thing that would worry you most is a patient realising they're talking to something automatic. Is that fair?

**Then stop.** The silence after this is uncomfortable and you should let it sit. Owners frequently add the most important thing in the call in that gap — often something they didn't intend to say.

**When they ask "so what would you do?" — and they will:**
> I've got a fairly clear idea, but I'd rather not answer that off the top of my head — half of what you've told me I want to sit with, and a couple of things I'd want to check against what you've said. That's what the document is for. Thursday.

This is a small refusal and it does more for your credibility than an answer would. Everyone else answers immediately.

**Second's one question goes here** — the sharpest thing that came up and got left.

---

## [47:00–50:00] Next step, dated

> Here's what happens next. By Thursday I'll send you a short document — two or three pages. What I heard, the three things that look like they're costing you the most, ranked, and which one I'd do first and why.
>
> There's one thing I'd want from you before then: [ONE thing — see below].
>
> Then a short call, twenty minutes, to go through it and decide. Would Monday 4pm work?

**Book the decision call on this call.** "I'll send it and we'll talk after" is how a warm conversation becomes a follow-up sequence.

**The one thing.** Exactly one, and it must be producible in under ten minutes. In descending order of preference:
1. An export of the last 90 days of enquiries from wherever they're kept
2. A screenshot of the last month of WhatsApp Business stats
3. Their no-show count for last month, however roughly
4. A five-minute chat with their coordinator

**Do not send `08 Collateral/Audit Call Docs/02_data_intake_requirements.md` here.** Raw CRM export plus Meta read-only access plus billing counts plus a staff list, from a clinic that hasn't agreed to anything, is a document that ends conversations. It's the right document at Stage 4 kickoff. It is the wrong document today.

## Handling the two mid-call derailments

**"What does this cost?"** — asked at minute 12, usually.
> Happy to answer that now and then get back to your side of things. The pilot's free — two or three weeks on one thing so you can see whether it does anything. If you want to carry on after, setup is ₹20,000 and ₹40,000 a month. But I'd be guessing at what's worth doing until I've finished asking you this stuff.

Then return to the question you were on. Answer plainly, don't linger, don't get pulled into scoping.

**"Can you just show me the product?"**
Show the demo — two minutes, live, then back. Have them message the demo number themselves. Then: *"That's the mechanism. Whether any of it's worth doing at your clinic is what the rest of these questions are about."*

---

## Immediately after — 15 minutes, don't skip it

Before the next thing, both of you:

1. **Fill the divergence table** from `Audit_call_docs/03` with what the owner said. The coordinator column stays empty until Stage 4 — an empty column is a visible reminder that you've heard one side of the story.
2. **Write down the three most quotable things they said, verbatim.** Their words go in the findings document. Yours don't land the same way.
3. **Name the wedge.** One, from `wedge-signal-entry.md` §3, with the evidence line from the call attached. If two look equal, note both and decide when drafting — but ship one.
4. **Note what you couldn't confirm.** This becomes a real section of the document, not a hedge.
5. **Update the Notion row and `REPLY_LOG.csv`.**

**Self-check on the call itself:**
- Did you talk more than half the time? *(If yes, the call underperformed regardless of how it felt.)*
- Did you name a solution before minute 42?
- Did you get the inbox on screen, or a counted answer?
- Is the next step booked, with a date, on the calendar?
- Do you have three verbatim quotes?
