# 04 — Findings and Rollout Plan

**Owns:** Stage 3. The document that goes out 48–72 hours after the operations call, and the 20-minute call that follows it.

**What this document is:** the first time this clinic has ever seen their own operation written down by someone who looked at it carefully. That's the whole value, and it's real value whether or not they buy.

**What it is not:** a proposal. There is no scope table, no deliverables list, no signature line, no pricing annexure. The moment it looks like a proposal, it gets read like one — skimmed for the number at the bottom, and everything above it becomes preamble.

**Inherits from `One-page-docs/diagnostic_doc_playbook.md`** — the diagnostic pyramid, the three buckets, the never-invent-a-number rule, the clinical-case-file register. Read §3 and §5 of that file before drafting. The difference: the one-pager was written from outside with one verified fact. This is written after they told you how it actually works. It can be far more specific — and far more specific is exactly what makes it worth reading.

---

## Timing

**48–72 hours. Not same-day, not next week.**

Same-day says you had it written before the call. A week says you forgot. Two to three days reads as work having been done, which is also what actually happened.

**If it will be late, say so before it's late.** One line, the day before: *"Running a day behind on this — Friday rather than Thursday. Wanted to check one thing properly rather than guess at it."* Being a day late with a reason costs nothing. Silence past a promised date costs the deal, and it costs it on the exact dimension you sell.

---

## Structure

Two to three pages. Under 800 words. If it's longer, something is being explained that should be cut.

### Header
Clinic name, "Operations Findings," the date of the call. Same visual system as the one-pagers.

### 1 · What you told us
*(Their words. ~120 words.)*

Three or four direct quotes from the call, lightly cleaned, attributed as *"you said."* Nothing else. No commentary, no framing.

This section does more work than the rest of the document combined. Hearing your own words written down by someone who was listening carefully is disarming in a way that no analysis is. It also, quietly, establishes that the rest of the document is built on what they said rather than on what you assumed.

> You said a patient messaging at nine at night usually hears back the next morning, and that you're not certain how often that's happening — "there's no way for me to know, honestly."
>
> You said your coordinator tries "two, maybe three times" before closing an enquiry, and that where she tracks that is her own notebook.
>
> You said three or four out of every ten booked consults don't turn up, and that afterwards, "we just move on."
>
> On automation, you said the thing that would worry you is "a patient feeling like they're talking to a machine when they're asking about their skin."

**Rules:** verbatim or near-verbatim only. Never improve their phrasing. If you're not sure they said it, cut it — one misquoted line makes every other line suspect.

### 2 · What we could confirm from outside
*(~80 words.)*

The externally verified facts from the pre-outbound audit: the timed mystery shop, the ad activity, the review pattern. Facts only, with dates. This is the section that proves you did work before they ever spoke to you.

### 3 · What we still can't see
*(~80 words.)*

The honest gaps. Named plainly, not hedged.

> We still can't see: how many enquiries actually arrive in a month across all channels, how many of your no-shows ever rebook, and what happens to a quote after it's been given. None of that is visible from outside, and from the call it doesn't look like it's fully visible from inside either — which is itself worth noting.

**This is the most counter-intuitive section and the most important one.** Every vendor this clinic has spoken to arrived certain. A stranger listing the limits of what he knows is doing something they've not seen, and it's the section that makes the confident sections believable.

### 4 · The three things, ranked
*(~250 words. The analytical core.)*

Three findings, ranked by what they appear to cost. Each one climbs the pyramid from `diagnostic_doc_playbook.md` §3 — evidence, observation, insight — in three or four sentences.

> **1. Quotes go out and nothing follows them.**
> You said quotes go out most weeks and that a patient "comes back if they decide." There's no list of who's been quoted and hasn't decided. So a patient who was genuinely interested but got busy is indistinguishable from one who said no — both simply stop appearing. The cost isn't the follow-up that didn't happen; it's that nobody at the clinic can name a single patient this happened to, which is why it's never been treated as a problem.
>
> **2. Consults that don't happen leave no trace.**
> Three or four in ten don't turn up, and afterwards nothing happens. A missed consult is the only point in the whole journey where the patient has already chosen you and already committed time — and it's the point with the least structure behind it.
>
> **3. The after-hours window is spent on a promise, not a question.**
> [...]

**Rules, all hard:**
- **Never state what any of this costs in rupees.** Not a range, not "conservatively." One invented number makes a sharp reader silently doubt every true line — and doctors are sharp readers of exactly this. `diagnostic_doc_playbook.md` §2.
- Each finding traces to something they said or something verified. Nothing from a "possible gaps" checklist. `MEMORY.md` §2.
- Rank by cost, not by what you'd enjoy building.
- Where a finding is uncertain, say so inside the finding rather than in a footnote.

### 5 · What we'd do first
*(~150 words.)*

**One thing.** Not three. Not a phased everything.

> Of the three, we'd start with the quote follow-up — for one reason. Those patients have already been to your clinic, already met you, and already been given a price. They're the warmest people you have, and they're the group nobody is currently talking to. Nothing else on the list starts from that much goodwill.
>
> Two to three weeks. Free. Here's the shape:
>
> **Week 1** — we take the quotes from the last 90 days and build the list that doesn't currently exist. You see it before anything is sent.
> **Week 2** — messages go out to that list, in your clinic's voice, approved by you before a single one sends. Anyone who replies goes straight to [named coordinator], not to us.
> **Week 3** — we look at what came back together, and you decide whether any of it was worth your time.

**Why one wedge:** `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §5 — sell one wedge only; expansion comes from delivery data, never from a pitch. A three-part plan also gives three chances to object, and a clinic that has to evaluate three things evaluates none of them.

### 6 · What we'd need from you
*(~60 words. Deliberately small.)*

> Three things, and none of them are big:
> 1. The quotes given in the last 90 days — a list, a sheet, or a photo of the register. However it exists.
> 2. One person we can reach who can answer within a few working hours when something needs a decision.
> 3. Half an hour of your time to approve the message wording before anything goes out.
>
> Nothing leaves your systems. No patient information goes anywhere except back to your own team.

**Still not the moment for `Audit_call_docs/02`.** The full intake list belongs at kickoff, after a yes, delivered in a setup session rather than as a document. Sending it now converts a warm clinic into a cold one.

### 7 · If it doesn't work
*(~50 words.)*

> If nothing comes back from those messages, you owe nothing and we'll show you exactly where it stalled — which list, which message, which point people stopped. That's genuinely useful to you either way, and it's the only thing we can honestly promise.

Per `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §5: a named guarantee with a modest, specific outcome. **Never a patient count. Never a revenue figure.** The guarantee is transparency about failure, not a promise of success — and in a market where everyone promises numbers, that's the more credible instrument.

### Close
One line, and the date of the already-booked call.

> That's everything. Monday at 4 — twenty minutes, and mostly your questions.

## The decision call (20 minutes)

**Structure:**
- **0–2:** *"Did anything in there not match how it actually works?"* Open with the possibility that you were wrong. It's the fastest route to the real objection.
- **2–8:** Their questions. Answer them and stop talking. Resist elaborating.
- **8–14:** The two objections that will come, handled below.
- **14–18:** If yes — kickoff date, named POC, setup session booked on the call.
- **18–20:** What happens in the next 48 hours, specifically.

**The two objections that actually come:**

**"Let me discuss it with my partner / think about it."**
Real, usually. Don't fight it. Do put a date on it.
> Of course. Would it help if I sent a two-line version they could read in a minute — they haven't been on these calls. And can I check back Thursday, or is next week better?

**"What's the catch, if it's free?"**
The best question they can ask and it deserves a straight answer.
> Two things, honestly. We need a first clinic we can point to — if this works, we'll ask whether you'd be willing to say so. And we're only doing this for a small number of clinics, because it's the two of us actually doing the work.
>
> The catch on your side is time, not money. If nobody at your end replies when a patient does, this doesn't work and we'd rather stop than let it run badly.

**If it's a no:** accept cleanly, no push, no "can I ask why" (`OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §4). Then ask one thing:
> Completely fine. One thing if you don't mind — was there a point in this where it stopped being interesting? Only asking because we're early and I'd genuinely rather know.

Log the answer in `REPLY_LOG.csv` under `stage_lost_at`. At this stage, a clear no with a reason is worth more than three maybes.

## Self-check before sending

1. Every claim traceable to something they said or something verified? *(`MEMORY.md` §2.)*
2. Any rupee figure, patient count, or percentage that wasn't given by them? *(Cut it.)*
3. Exactly one thing proposed first — not two, not phased?
4. Is the data ask one item, producible in ten minutes?
5. Does §1 use their actual words, unimproved?
6. Under 800 words? *(Try cutting a fifth. If it survives, it wasn't ready — `diagnostic_doc_playbook.md` §8.)*
7. Any banned vocabulary — leads, funnel, conversion, ROI, growth, scale? *(`OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §3.)*
8. Would a clinic owner say these sentences out loud? *(`MEMORY.md` §2.)*
9. Is the decision call already booked, with a date in the document?
