# THE BUYER, THE OFFER, AND WHAT CAN BE DEMONSTRATED

---

## 1 · Who you are writing to

Not a generic SMB owner. Calibrate everything against this:

- **Doctor-first identity.** They see themselves as clinicians, not business operators. "Scale your
  practice," "leads," "funnel," "pipeline," "conversion" all read as someone who doesn't understand
  their world. Speak in **patients, consult, booking, follow-up, walk-in**.
- **Already burned by an agency.** The most common pattern among Indian clinic owners is having paid
  for marketing before and received fake reviews, junk leads, or a guaranteed-patient-count promise
  that didn't hold. Assume this history. **Never say it out loud** — it shapes tone, it never appears
  in copy.
- **WhatsApp is the real operating system.** Instagram is the discovery and credibility surface —
  they live there to be *seen* — but the actual back-and-forth with patients happens on WhatsApp.
  Earn the move from IG to WhatsApp; don't assume it.
- **Trust = competence, then warmth, fast.** A sharp factual observation proves competence. A human,
  founder-to-founder tone proves warmth. Both are needed in the first message: fact alone reads as
  surveillance, warmth alone reads as another empty DM.
- **Specificity is the entire credibility mechanism.** Their feed is full of agencies with generic
  compliments. A vague compliment is *worse than silence* — it actively signals mass-sender.
- **Never name a guaranteed number.** "100 patients in 30 days" is the exact script every scam in this
  space uses. Naming a number is the credibility *killer* here — the opposite of how this instinct
  usually works elsewhere.
- **The real objection is never price. It's exposure** — the fear of still paying for something dead
  eight months from now.

---

## 2 · The offer

**Settled shape (confirmed 13 Aug 2026):**

> **The audit and the strategy document are free. Building and running the system is paid.**

- Nothing is charged before the clinic has read the findings document.
- **Paid path:** ₹20,000 setup, ₹40,000/month, one month's notice, no lock-in, no annual contract.
  That conversation happens *after* the free document, never before.
- **Never a guaranteed patient count.** Ever.
- **Named guarantee (the compliant pattern):** *"If we don't recover [modest, specific outcome], you
  owe nothing and I'll show you exactly where it stalled."*
- **Rev-share is banned at cold stage.** It sounds like risk-sharing and reads as a stranger claiming
  a piece of their revenue.
- **₹2,000 refundable deposit — reactivation pilots only.** Never a first cold clinic, and never in
  client-facing copy as a universal ask.
- **Sell one wedge only.** The full system is the expansion, never the opener.

**⚠️ Unresolved — ask before quoting anything:** whether the *free 2–3 week pilot* (long-settled: free,
time-boxed, run on the clinic's existing backlog) still exists as a distinct step, or has collapsed
into the free audit + document. "Implementation is paid" and "the pilot is free" both describe running
a flow against real patients.

**Known open problem, don't paper over it:** free has been offered ~25 times and accepted zero times.
That is not a pricing signal — it means the value isn't legible, and "free" from an unknown sender
reads as a pitch in disguise. **The fix is a sharper, cheaper ask, not a price.** A paid ₹7,500
tripwire was proposed and **explicitly rejected** — don't re-propose it without new evidence.

## 3 · What can and cannot be demonstrated

The demo is **built and live**. Know its edges and state them unprompted.

| Capability | How it's shown |
|---|---|
| **Instant reply · qualification · booking into the diary** | **LIVE.** Hand them the number and let them message it themselves, at whatever hour they like. With zero clients this is the only real evidence that exists — *a claim they can test is not a claim.* |
| **No-show recovery · quote-decay chase · dormant reactivation** | **Walked through as the actual message sequences.** Not demoed live. |

**Say the second row out loud rather than letting them find it.** Those flows only mean anything
running against real bookings and a real missed appointment; staging a scenario per clinic doesn't
scale, and a staged no-show proves nothing.

Framed honestly — *"I'm not going to fake that one for you"* — the limitation reads as **method
transparency**, which is the only honest differentiation available to a company with no clients.
Framed badly, a founder messages the demo asking about no-shows and finds nothing.

## 4 · The positioning, and why it's shaped this way

Zero clients, zero results, no case study. Fixed for now, and no framing changes it.

What is *not* zero: **research depth.** Nobody else in this market mystery-shops a clinic, times the
reply, reads a spread of reviews, checks the ad library, and arrives with a timestamped finding before
asking for anything.

Every competitor leads with claimed results. The market has been burned by exactly that. So the only
honest differentiation is **method transparency** — showing exactly how the work gets done, on their
own clinic, before any money changes hands, and being explicit about what isn't known yet.

**The corollary, which is the whole position:** being early is not a weakness to hide until asked.
It *is* the offer.

> *"Client one gets two founders who cannot afford to fail. Client forty at an agency gets a junior
> account manager."*

Say it once, plainly, volunteered — never extracted under questioning. Volunteered honesty about stage
reads as confidence; the same fact admitted late reads as concealment. **Unproven, but it is the bet.**

## 5 · The ask ladder

**Drop-in first. Call as the accommodation. On-site once the audit is agreed.**

A call is the most expensive thing you can ask a clinician for — a block carved out of a day already
full of patients. 25 one-pagers → 2 replies → **0 calls**, and every one of those assets terminated in
a call or a free audit.

```
I'm around [area] Thursday — happy to swing by for ten minutes and show you what I found.
Or if that's more than you want to take on right now, a quick call works just as well.
```

Two asks that cost them nothing, and should come before any call:
- *"Here's a number — message it the way a patient would."* (The demo. Live now.)
- *"I'm around [area] Thursday — happy to swing by for ten minutes."*

**Epistemic status: unproven.** Zero calls and zero meetings have been held. The reasoning is sound and
this is the current approach — but it is not an evidenced pattern, and it must never be described to a
clinic as something other clinics prefer.

## 6 · What happens after a positive reply

Five stages, each with a clock. **Designed 4 Aug, never run — treat all of it as provisional.**

| # | Stage | Clock | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Reply triage | ≤4 business hours | Reply sent + slots proposed |
| 1 | Credibility packet | ≤24h | Packet delivered + call booked with a date |
| 2 | Operations call (50 min) | ≤5 days | Diagnosis captured, next step dated live |
| 3 | Findings + rollout doc | 48–72h after call | Doc sent + 20-min decision call booked |
| 4 | Build + onboarding | Kickoff ≤7 days after yes | Setup done, visible artifact live by day 3 |

**Three rules that hold it together:**
1. **Nothing ships without a date attached.** Every asset travels with a booked time or two named
   slots. A document without a date is a stall you built yourself. *(This is exactly how Sapphire
   stalled.)*
2. **Diagnosis and prescription never happen in the same conversation.** The call diagnoses; the
   document prescribes. A call that must end with a plan pressures you toward a plan you already had.
3. **A warm reply outranks the send quota.** Always.

**Three reply types, three moves:**

| Type | Looks like | What they're really asking | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Curiosity** | "Good observation." "How did you find this?" | *Is there more where that came from?* | Answer briefly and honestly, then offer the walkthrough + two slots |
| **B — Credential check** | "Send your company profile / pricing." "Which clients do you work with?" | *Are you real, and will this waste my time?* | Send the packet **with** two slots. **Never the document alone.** |
| **C — Ready** | "Call me." "When can we talk?" | Nothing — they're in | Book immediately, packet as pre-work |

**Type B is what broke on Sapphire and is most likely to break again.** The instinct is to treat it as
a document request. It isn't — it's a credential check *and* a soft stall.

**The single most important instruction on the call: do not solve anything.** The urge to prove
competence by fixing something live is the strongest failure mode here. It feels like the call going
well, and it empties the follow-up document of any reason to exist.
