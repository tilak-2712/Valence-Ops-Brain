# QUALIFICATION, THE MYSTERY SHOP, AND WEDGE ROUTING

---

## 0 · The core rule

The entry wedge is **diagnosed, never pre-selected.** For every clinic, find the earliest *provable*
stage where money is visibly dying, and lead with the offer that fixes that stage. Dead-lead
reactivation is one wedge among many — never the default.

When several breaks exist, prioritise the one that is:
1. **Screenshot-provable**
2. **Closest to money already spent**
3. **Fastest to show a visible win**

Enter on **one wedge only.** The full system is the expansion, never the opener.

> ### ⚠️ The standing warning — flagged four times and still happening
> The speed-to-lead wedge keeps getting forced onto clinics the shop proved are **fast** — by
> conceding the fact and substituting an abstraction: *"the reply is fast, that's not the gap, the
> gap is…"* That reframe is structurally weaker: it gives up the concrete verifiable fact and
> replaces it with something the founder cannot check. All 15 one-pagers diagnose a variant of the
> same thing. **The routing table below has 14 rows and one is doing all the work.**
> **If the shop says fast, find a genuinely different wedge or don't send.**

## 1 · Hard kills — disqualify immediately

| # | Signal | Determinable before outreach? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No website **and** no Instagram **and** no Google Business Profile | Yes |
| 2 | Operating under 12 months (oldest Google review) | Yes |
| 3 | Inbound volume under ~20 enquiries/month | **No** — mark `undetermined`, never guess |
| 4 | Average treatment value below ₹25,000 | Partly — inferable from advertised pricing; label as estimate |
| 5 | Committee / multi-partner sign-off, no single decision-maker | Yes |
| 6 | Owner fully detached from operations, feels no pain | **No** — a discovery-call finding |
| 7 | Primary ask is lead-gen, ads, website or content → **refer out** | Yes |
| 8 | No enquiry data exists anywhere | **No** — a discovery-call finding |
| 9 | Passes both speed-to-lead tests | **No** — post-shop only |
| **10** | **No named decision-maker reachable on a personal channel** | **Yes — and it costs two minutes** |

**On #10:** this used to sit *after* the most expensive step. Three complete dossiers were written for
clinics with no doctor named anywhere before anyone noticed. Check it at the start.

**On #3, #4, #6, #8:** four of ten cannot be determined before outreach. Mark them `undetermined` and
move on — don't infer them, and don't let an `undetermined` quietly become a pass in a summary table.

**Park, don't kill:**
- **Mega founder-brand** (very large personal following) → deferred to *after* Case Study #1.
  Gatekeepers and likely incumbent agencies make them a worse bet for a first client than a
  founder-run mid-tier clinic.
- **Shallow existing automation** (greeting-only auto-reply, no qualification) → High priority. The
  wedge is a depth upgrade, not a new build.
- **Front desk is the only reachable contact** → route via Practo doctor listing, website team page,
  or LinkedIn before killing.

**Explicitly NOT disqualifiers:** not running Meta ads (check Practo Prime / JustDial paid listings
and Google Ads Transparency instead — a Meta-only check has wrongly reported "no ads" in *every*
batch) · missing one channel · a messy CRM · strong surface reputation with no visible weakness (the
shop decides, not first impressions).

## 2 · The mystery shop

**Tilak runs every shop personally. No agent ever sends a message to a clinic, for any reason.**

**The shop opens the batch — it does not close it.** It is the only 24–72h async dependency in the
process, and running it last is how one cohort produced 25 dossiers and zero shops. Queue rule:
*async dependencies open the batch.* Monday: ~20 shops in one sitting. Tue–Thu: research only the
clinics whose threads came back interesting. You cannot over-research a clinic you haven't shopped.

### 2.1 Admissibility — the rule that governs everything

> **An enquiry is admissible as a hook if, and only if, it lands inside the hours the clinic publishes
> on its own Google profile.**

- **Kills midnight permanently** without needing a separate ban. *"We were closed"* is a correct
  rebuttal, and leading with it signals you don't understand how a clinic runs.
- **Opens Saturday, Sunday and weekday evenings.** Across 171 aesthetic/derm clinics in the Bangalore
  scrape: **Saturday 6pm is the highest published-open slot at 92%**, Tuesday 6pm at 88%, Tuesday 2pm
  only 78%. **220 of 223** clinics with published hours are open Saturday; 158 are open Sunday.
  **Weekend is not after-hours in this market** — it's when a patient buying a ₹1.5L procedure has
  time to enquire, and when the desk is thinnest.
- **Also kills some tests the old fixed window permitted.** Many clinics run split shifts
  (`10 AM–1 PM, 4:30–8:30 PM`), so an 11am–4pm window sat on top of the midday closure.
- **Free upgrade:** the sharpest slot isn't their busiest hour — an ignored enquiry at peak invites
  *"we were slammed."* It's a **published-open, low-traffic hour.** Nothing to explain there.

### 2.2 The two tests

Both fire inside a single thread, from one message.

| # | Test | How | If it fails → wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Qualification** | In their reply, did anyone ask about concern, treatment interest, budget or timeline — or did they just answer your question? | Qualification + scoring layer |
| 2 | **Quote decay** | Ask a price on a high-ticket procedure, receive it, go quiet. Does anyone chase? | Quote-decay follow-up |

On a ₹49,999–₹1.5L procedure, **an unchased quote is the most expensive silence you can prove**, and
it costs one message plus silence.

**Retired tests — don't reinstate without a decision:**
- **After-hours (10–11pm)** — the rebuttal is correct, every clinic fails it so it ranks nothing, and
  its output is inadmissible anyway. *Narrow carve-out: confirmed heavy ad-spenders only, touch-2
  material only, framed as spend against staffing — never as slowness.*
- **Cross-channel (IG vs WhatsApp speed)** — WhatsApp is the channel that matters. A slow IG DM costs
  the clinic little if real volume happens on WhatsApp.
- **Persistence (7–14 days)** — mostly tells you what quote decay tells you sooner.
- **Booking friction** — requires actually booking. Expensive, and burns the test identity.

**Still worth doing, as an observation not a test:** the auto-reply text. What matters is whether the
automation *asks anything* or just promises a callback. One message, one screenshot, no waiting.

### 2.3 Save the screenshot, not the summary

**The timestamped image is the deliverable** (`clinic-YYYYMMDD-test.png`). Prose is derived from it,
never the reverse. Shop results living only as paraphrase inside a dossier is precisely how a generic
template line once became an entire clinic's wedge.

### 2.4 Verdict

- **Fails one or both** → the failure identifies the wedge. Run it through the rebuttal test.
- **Passes both** → hard kill #9. Log and move on; don't keep probing.

### 2.5 When the clinic has a fast *human* responder

Don't sell speed — they have it and are usually proud of it. **Sell fragility and cost:**

> *"This works because of one person. What happens on their day off, after 8pm, or if they quit? And
> how much of their day goes to typing the same reply to people who were never going to book?"*

Keep their closer; remove the chasing. Nothing about how they close changes.

## 3 · Signal → wedge routing

| Signal combination | Entry wedge | Opening frame |
|---|---|---|
| Ads ✓ · response slow or none | Dead-lead reactivation | "You already paid for these enquiries. They're dying in your inbox." |
| Ads ✓ · fast first reply · no qualification, no follow-up | Follow-up / nurture engine | "You won the first 5 minutes and lost the next 14 days." |
| Ads ✓ · thin GBP · fast human reply | Review engine first, infra second | "Your ads send strangers to a thin profile — trust leaks before the message arrives." |
| Ads ✓ · greeting-only auto-reply · no qualification | Qualification + scoring upgrade | "The bot says hi. Nobody finds out who's worth calling first." |
| No ads · website ✓ · high reviews · slow response | Instant response + organic capture | "Your reputation markets for free. Your inbox kills what it earns." |
| No ads · high review count, last review months old | Review reactivation | "Your best patients left quietly. The proof engine stalled months ago." |
| No ads · multi-session treatments · 12+ months operating | Dormant-patient reactivation | "These people already trusted you enough to walk in once. They're sitting in your WhatsApp history." |
| Practo Prime / JustDial paid · slow response | Dead-lead reactivation (aggregator) | "You pay Practo for enquiries, then leave them on read. Same leak, different bill." |
| Reviews mention booking friction, waits, ghosting after payment | No-show recovery | "Every empty chair this month was a booked patient last week." |
| High-ticket quote given, never chased | Quote-decay follow-up | "An unchased ₹1.5L quote is the most expensive silence in the clinic." |
| Fast human · manual chasing · one person holds it all | Systematise the hustle | "Keep your closer. Remove the chasing. What happens after 8pm, or on their day off?" |
| Replies fast then sends them to a website / link dump | **Enquiry deflection** | "You answered in under a minute — and sent me to a website." |
| Mega founder brand · overflow signals | Founder capacity protection | "You solved attention. The leak is between the message and the chair." |
| Multiple breaks at once | Earliest provable break wins | Enter on one wedge only |

**Retired row:** *WhatsApp fast · Instagram slow → IG→WhatsApp handoff.* Killed after being led with
twice. A cross-channel speed comparison is only a wedge if the *slow* channel is the one the business
runs on — and here that's WhatsApp, not Instagram.

**Before any of these ship, run the rebuttal test.** Several frames above ("You run 25 ads") are
one-sentence-rebuttable as written and need a first-party fact attached to survive.

## 4 · Review reading — how to do it properly

Never report bare count + rating.

- **Pull chronological, 25+ minimum.** Small samples on Google's *relevance* sort make "no complaints
  found" a worthless statement — it's an artifact of the sort order, not a fact about the clinic.
- **Report the full distribution** (5★/4★/3★/2★/1★), not the average.
- **Read the negatives.** Report count, % of the pull rated ≤2★, and the **owner reply rate among
  negatives** — 0/5 replied is a materially different clinic from 6/6 replied. Reply *tone* matters
  too: defensive, combative and templated are each a distinct finding.
- **Recurring complaint shapes across independent reviewers** are far sharper evidence than one
  complaint. The dominant shape in this market: **"paid in full upfront, then silence."**
- If *all* reviews are years old, that stall **is itself the finding** — don't report "no recent
  complaints" as if it were health.
- **Escalation-grade allegations** (negligence, fraud, legal notice, physical harm) get surfaced
  prominently, never smoothed into surrounding prose.
