## 0 · Verdict, before the list

1. **GST is not the blocker.** You can sign, invoice and start building without a GSTIN. Section 9 explains why, and why RCM is the opposite of the problem you think it is.
2. **The blocker is §7 of the SkinFit tech handoff — the six commitments, especially #3.** You have put in writing to a founder that you only message people who contacted the clinic first, "within what WhatsApp and Meta permit." That sentence is only true on the official WhatsApp Business API through a BSP. Adi has not confirmed it. If it goes out unconfirmed, the first written statement you ever made to a client is a compliance claim you cannot back. That is a bigger legal exposure than 18% IGST.
3. **Do not send an MSA to SkinFit.** They have not agreed to the audit, and the buyer (Israni) has not been met. The next document that gets signed is a **one-page audit access and confidentiality letter**, not a contract set. Building the full set now is correct preparation; sending it now would kill the deal.
4. **There is no company.** Valence Ops is a proprietorship: the legal person is S Tilak, contracts are signed "S Tilak, Proprietor, trading as Valence Ops," there is no CIN, no board resolution and no company seal with legal force. **Liability is personal and unlimited** — which raises the stakes on the liability cap, the medical disclaimer and the platform-compliance clause in §5.1, because those now protect Tilak's own assets rather than a corporate shell.
5. **The largest legal gap in this project is not client-facing.** Four people are described as founders (`MEMORY.md` — Tilak, Pratham, Maddy, Adi). A proprietorship means **Tilak legally owns 100% of the business, the client relationships, the IP and the revenue, and the other three own nothing.** No founder agreement exists. This does not block SkinFit and should not delay it. It is the thing that becomes expensive later. See §2.6.
6. **You do not need the current account to apply for GST.** Correcting what this file said in its first pass. See §9.4.
7. **Minimum viable legal set to take money and start work: four documents.** Everything else in this file is either a company registration you need anyway, or a document that makes you harder to argue with later. The four are marked ★.

### 2.2 Identity and money
1. **PAN — Tilak's personal PAN.** A proprietorship has no separate PAN. The clinic deducts TDS against it, and the credit appears in Tilak's own Form 26AS. Business income is taxed at individual slab rates, not corporate rates. Factor that into any pricing maths.
2. **TAN** — only needed when you start deducting TDS yourself. Not needed to receive money.
3. **Current account in the name "Valence Ops."** In flight. Once it exists, **never take a client payment into a personal savings account.** With a proprietorship the money is legally Tilak's either way, but mixing the two destroys the audit trail and makes the first client's invoice unreconcilable.
4. **Cancelled cheque or bank letter** — clinics ask for this to open you as a vendor.
5. **Name board, rubber stamp, address stamp.** Required by the bank, customary on invoices. **The stamp carries no legal force for a proprietorship** — Tilak's signature is what binds. Do not let anyone treat a stamped-but-unsigned document as executed.

### 2.3 ⚠️ Two document inconsistencies to fix before the GST application

### 2.4 Registrations still open

### 2.6 The founder-equity gap — read this even though it does not block SkinFit

14. **Legally, Tilak owns everything.** The clients, the contracts, the revenue, the code, the WhatsApp system, the name. Pratham, Maddy and Adi have no ownership, no defined share, and no claim on anything they build.
15. **Nothing about this is urgent and nothing about it should slow SkinFit down.** Incorporating now would reset the bank account, the GST application and the registration set by three to four weeks, at exactly the moment the first real deal is live. That trade is not worth it.
16. **What is worth doing now, and costs a weekend:** a written founders' understanding covering equity split, what happens on incorporation, who owns the IP, vesting, and what happens if someone leaves. Not a filed document, not a lawyer job at this stage. It exists so that the conversation happens while nobody has anything to lose by having it.
17. **The trigger to actually incorporate:** the first signed paying client, or the first outside money, whichever comes first. At that point the arguments flip — a Pvt Ltd caps personal liability on health-adjacent patient data, makes the equity split real, and is what any subsequent investor or larger client will expect. `provisional` — this is a judgement, not a rule.
18. **Until then, price the risk in.** Unlimited personal liability means the liability cap, the medical disclaimer and the platform-compliance warranty in §5.1 are not boilerplate. They are the only protection Tilak has.

4. **The de-risking that the audit used to do now has to be done by the contract.** That is the whole legal consequence of this path, and §3A.4 is where it lands.

### 3A.2 Documents that go out — lighter than §3
5. **The Information Request itself**, tiered and split by respondent. Not the full 55 questions in one document.
6. **A covering message carrying the date.** Named respondent, return-by date, what happens next and when. `SALES_MOTION.md` Rule 1 applies harder here than anywhere, because a questionnaire with no deadline is the Sapphire stall rebuilt.
7. ~~**Audit Access & Confidentiality Letter**~~ — **no longer needed.** There is no access to govern. This is a real simplification: one of the four blocking documents just disappeared.

Four lines: what you will use it for, that you will not share it, that you are not asking for patient data, and that they can ask you to delete it at any time.
9. **One added sentence on screenshots:** *"If a screenshot would show real patient names or numbers, please blur them or send a blank record — we don't need any patient data."* Demonstrates commitment #1 and #6 instead of asserting them.

14. **No claim that an audit was performed.** Including softer versions — "our review found," "our analysis shows." You reviewed what they told you plus what you tested from outside. Say exactly that.

### 3A.4 What the proposal must gain — the contract does the audit's job now
15. **An Assumptions Schedule, attached and signed with the SOW.** Every client statement the scope depends on, listed. For SkinFit that will include: what practice software is in use, whether a booking can be written into it or only requested, where ad-form submissions arrive, whether WhatsApp is on the official API or the Business app, how many numbers are in use, and whether the two branches share a system.
16. **A re-scope trigger clause.** If a listed assumption is materially wrong at onboarding, scope and price are revisited before build starts, with a stated window (say five working days) and a clean exit for both sides if you cannot agree. **Without this you have fixed-priced a build against facts nobody verified.** This is the single most important commercial consequence of skipping the audit.
17. **A paid validation phase, days 1–3 of onboarding.** Not free discovery — the first three days of the engagement are spent verifying the answers against reality before anything is built. Priced in. This does not breach the free-audit promise made in outbound, because it sits after a signature, not before one.
18. **A migration contingency.** If WhatsApp turns out to be on the Business app rather than the official API, number migration is a dependency you do not control, with its own timeline. Name it in the schedule now rather than explaining it in week two.
19. **Platform-dependency clause.** Template approval times, WhatsApp quality rating, and messaging tier are Meta's decisions, not yours. Timelines are stated as subject to them.
20. **A tighter client-dependency clause than usual.** On this path you are more reliant on them producing access and answers after signature than you would be after an audit. State what you need, by when, and that the timeline moves if it arrives late.

## 5 · Stage C — Yes → the agreement set

Five documents. Only two are strictly necessary; the rest are cheap and remove arguments.

| # | Document | Necessary? | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ★ **Services Agreement (MSA)** | Yes | The master terms. Signed once, survives multiple projects |
| 2 | ★ **Schedule A — Scope of Work** | Yes | The specific engagement. Usually just the proposal, attached and signed |
| 3 | **Schedule B — Data Processing Agreement** | Effectively yes | DPDP requires the Fiduciary to engage a Processor under contract. Health-adjacent data makes this non-optional in practice |
| 4 | **Schedule C — Service Levels** | Recommended | Your response times, and **their** four-business-hour reply SLA on routed patient messages |
| 5 | **Mutual NDA** | Only if asked | Usually redundant once the MSA has a confidentiality clause |

### 5.1 What must be inside the Services Agreement
1. **Parties, correctly named.** Your side: **"S Tilak, sole proprietor, trading as Valence Ops," with the registered address and the Udyam number.** No CIN — do not let a template put one there. Their side: a clinic often trades under a different name than its legal entity, and SkinFit has had at least two entities in play historically. **Ask for their CIN and check it on the MCA portal before signing.** A contract with the wrong SkinFit entity is unenforceable against the right one.
2. **Term and renewal.** Monthly rolling, one month's notice, no auto-lock.
3. **Fees, taxes, and invoicing schedule.** "Fees are exclusive of GST and other applicable taxes, which shall be borne by the Client." Put this in even while unregistered.
4. **TDS acknowledgement.** They will deduct tax at source (10% under 194J on professional/technical fees is the likely head — `verify` with your CA whether your service falls under 194J or 194C, the rates differ materially). Two clauses worth having: they deduct and deposit; they issue Form 16A quarterly. Chasing a missing Form 16A a year later is a real cost.
5. **Late payment.** Interest after N days, and the right to pause the service. A pause right you never use is still worth having.
6. **Third-party costs.** Meta conversation charges, BSP fees, cloud. Named, and whose card they sit on. If they sit on your card, they are pass-through at cost with the invoice attached.
7. **Ownership and licence.** Be deliberate here:
   - Their data, their patient list, their Meta assets, their WhatsApp number: **theirs**, always.
   - Your templates, flows, prompts, code, and the underlying system: **yours**, licensed to them for the term. Do not hand over IP on your first client. The clinic-specific configuration can be theirs.
   - What they keep on exit: their data, exports, and their own accounts. Not your system.
8. **Confidentiality**, mutual, surviving termination.
9. **The six commitments from the tech handoff §7, written into the contract**, not just the sales document. This is what makes them worth something. They were the strongest thing said in the meeting; leaving them in a PDF and out of the contract wastes them.
10. **Compliance and platform rules.** You operate within Meta and WhatsApp Business policy. The client warrants that the contacts they give you have a lawful basis for contact. This clause protects you if they hand you a purchased list.
11. **Medical disclaimer, explicit.** Nothing automated provides medical advice, diagnosis or treatment recommendation. No message is sent in a doctor's name without their approval. Non-negotiable in this vertical.
12. **Limitation of liability**, capped at fees paid in the preceding N months, with the usual carve-outs. **This is the most important clause in the document for you.** A proprietorship has no corporate shell — an uncapped liability runs straight to Tilak's personal assets. Do not accept a client redline that removes or widens the cap without understanding exactly what it exposes.
13. **No guarantee of outcome.** You may name a guarantee about *what you will do*; never a patient-count or revenue promise. `OUTBOUND_MEMORY.md` §5 already fixes this — mirror the same language in the contract.
14. **The stop-clause.** Three routed patient replies unanswered beyond 48 hours pauses the service. Already designed in `sales-motion/05`. Put it in the contract, where it protects both sides.
15. **Termination:** for convenience with notice; for cause on material breach with a cure period; immediate on a platform policy breach.
16. **Exit and transition.** Data export format, deletion timeline, access revocation. Write the divorce while everyone is friendly.
17. **Governing law and jurisdiction: Bengaluru, Karnataka.** Arbitration clause optional and probably overkill at this ticket size.
18. **Signature block.** Yours: *"S Tilak, Proprietor, for Valence Ops,"* with date. The rubber stamp is customary and legally irrelevant — **the signature is what binds, so never send a stamped-but-unsigned document.** Theirs: if the clinic is a Pvt Ltd, the signatory must be a director or someone holding a board authorisation. If a marketing head signs without authority, you have a contract you may not be able to enforce. **This applies directly to SkinFit** — Gino is not a decision maker, so he is not the signatory.

### 5.2 What must be inside the DPA
19. Categories of data processed — enquiry messages, names, phone numbers, stated treatment interest. **Explicitly not medical records.**
20. Purpose limitation — used only to operate the enquiry handling system for that clinic.
21. Sub-processors, named: the BSP, the cloud provider, any AI model provider. Clinics do ask where the data goes, and "I don't know" is the wrong answer.
22. Cross-border transfer position. Your stack is largely US-hosted. `verify` — DPDP permits transfer except to restricted territories, but the client's counsel will ask, and you should have the answer written before they do.
23. Security measures: access control, encryption in transit, who on your team can see what.
24. Breach notification: you tell them within N hours of becoming aware.
25. Retention and deletion on termination.
26. Their right to audit or ask for a report.

### 5.3 Authorisation letters — the ones people forget
27. **Meta / WhatsApp authorisation letter**, on the clinic's letterhead, signed by them, stating that ValenceOps is authorised to manage their WhatsApp Business Account and Messenger on their behalf. BSPs ask for this. Meta support asks for it when something breaks.
28. **Business verification documents from the clinic** — needed by Meta to verify their business: legal name, address, and a supporting document. Get these at signing, not when the number is already blocked.
29. **Opt-in evidence.** Your commitment #3 says you only message people who contacted them first. You need to be able to *show* how that is enforced — the referral parameter from the click-to-Messenger ad, the inbound message, the form submission. Document the mechanism once and reuse it.

## 6 · Stage D — Money, invoicing and tax paperwork

### 6.1 Before the first invoice
1. **Vendor onboarding form** — their format, filled by you. Expect: business name, PAN, GSTIN or a declaration of non-registration, bank details, cancelled cheque, MSME/Udyam certificate, address, POC, and proprietor KYC.
   - **Pre-empt the PAN question.** A clinic's accountant expecting a company PAN will query an individual one. Put it on the invoice as *"Valence Ops (Proprietor: S Tilak) · PAN: XXXXXXXXXX · Udyam: UDYAM-KR-03-0707297"* and it never comes up.
   - **Lead with the Udyam number.** Registered micro enterprise status obliges the buyer to pay within 45 days under the MSMED Act. Putting it on the invoice is free leverage with a clinic that drifts on payment.
2. ★ **A correct invoice template.** Two different formats depending on GST status:

| | **Without GSTIN** | **With GSTIN** |
|---|---|---|
| Header | Invoice (or Bill of Supply) | **Tax Invoice** |
| Must show | Entity name, address, CIN, PAN, invoice no. (sequential, unbroken), date, client name + address, description, amount, bank details | All of the left, **plus** your GSTIN, client's GSTIN, place of supply, HSN/SAC code, taxable value, CGST/SGST split, total |
| Must **not** show | Any GST amount, any GSTIN, the words "Tax Invoice" | — |
| Line to add | "Not registered under GST" | — |

**Charging GST without a registration is an offence.** Not a technicality — do not let a client-facing template carry a tax line "for now."

3. **Payment receipt / acknowledgement** on receipt of the setup fee. Small thing, reads professional.
4. **TDS tracking.** They pay you net of TDS. Reconcile against Form 26AS quarterly; chase Form 16A. The gap between invoiced and received is not a shortfall, it is your own tax credit sitting with the government.

### 6.2 If pass-through costs exist
5. Meta conversation charges and BSP fees billed at cost, with the underlying invoice attached each month. Never marked up silently. If you want margin on it, say so in the proposal and call it a platform fee.

## 9 · GST — the actual answer

### 9.1 Can you proceed without it? Yes.
1. **Threshold for services in Karnataka is ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover in a financial year.** Below that, registration is voluntary. `verify` with your CA against your projected turnover.
2. **You can sign contracts, raise invoices and receive payment with no GSTIN.** The invoice simply carries no tax line and states you are unregistered.
3. **The client side is fine.** A GST-registered clinic paying an unregistered vendor for services has no reverse-charge liability. The general Section 9(4) reverse charge on purchases from unregistered suppliers is not in force for this; it applies only to specifically notified goods and services. `verify`
4. **The practical friction, not the legal one:** a clinic with a real accountant may prefer a GST invoice, because paying an unregistered vendor gives them no input credit. At ₹20k–₹40k a month this is an irritation, not a deal-breaker. Nobody has ever raised it with you.

### 9.2 RCM on Meta — your concern is inverted
This is the part worth reading twice.

5. **Unregistered (where you are today).** Services like Meta/WhatsApp, cloud, and AI APIs supplied from outside India to an unregistered Indian recipient are OIDAR services. For those, the **foreign supplier is required to register in India and charge you GST** — you have no reverse-charge liability at all. Meta charges Indian advertisers without a GSTIN exactly this way. **You pay 18%, you cannot claim it back, and it is a pure cost.**
6. **Registered.** RCM applies: you self-assess 18% IGST on the import and **claim it back as input credit** in the same return, provided your output is taxable. Net cash effect roughly zero, plus a compliance step.
7. **So GST registration makes the Meta cost cheaper, not more expensive.** RCM is not the trap. Being unregistered while spending on US platforms is the trap, because the 18% is unrecoverable.
8. **The one genuine RCM trap to know about.** Section 24(iii) makes registration **mandatory, with no turnover threshold**, for anyone liable to pay tax under reverse charge. That bites on imported services that are *not* OIDAR — for example a foreign consultant, contractor, or a bespoke service contract from abroad. If you engage anything like that, the threshold stops protecting you. `verify` — this classification is exactly the question to put to your CA in one sentence.
9. **The clean workaround, available immediately:** buy WhatsApp conversations and the messaging platform through an **Indian BSP** (AiSensy, Interakt, Gupshup, Wati's India entity and similar). The invoice becomes a domestic supply with CGST+SGST, and the import/RCM question mostly disappears. You need a BSP anyway for commitment #3 to be true. **This single decision solves the compliance question and the platform-legitimacy question at the same time.** `provisional` — depends on Adi's answer in tech handoff §9.

### 9.3 Where GST actually does matter
10. **Meta Business Verification — this matters more for a proprietorship than it would for a company.** You have no Certificate of Incorporation, so your accepted documents are the **Udyam certificate, the S&E certificate, a GST certificate, or a business bank statement.** Udyam plus S&E should be sufficient. `verify` against Meta's accepted-document list at the time you apply. Two practical points: the **legal name and address must match across whatever you submit** (see §2.3), and a GST certificate is the single cleanest document to hand Meta if you have one. This is a real argument for registering, not just a tax argument.
11. **Once registered, you cannot go quiet.** Monthly or quarterly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, filed even in a nil month. Late fees accrue per day per return. The commitment is ongoing, which is the only real argument for waiting.

### 9.4 Timeline
12. Straightforward applications with clean Aadhaar authentication are typically processed in about 7 working days; without it, or when flagged, it goes to physical verification and takes considerably longer.
13. **Karnataka requires biometric Aadhaar authentication at a GST Suvidha Kendra** for many applicants, which means a booked appointment and a physical visit. **Plan for two to three weeks, not one.** `verify` — rules and state rollouts change; confirm current position with your CA.
14. **⚠️ Correcting the first pass of this file: you do not need the current account to apply.** Bank details are furnished *after* registration under Rule 10A (within 30 days of registration or before the first GSTR-1, whichever is earlier), not at the application stage. `verify` with your CA. **For a proprietorship you already hold everything the application needs today:** PAN, Aadhaar, photo, and proof of principal place of business — the S&E certificate plus the property document or NOC.
15. **The one thing that must be fixed first is §2.3.** A GST application whose address does not match its address proof gets pushed to physical verification, which is what turns a 7-day process into a 4-week one. Reconcile the address strings and get a correct signed NOC for #35/2 JK Nilaya **before** filing, not after a query arrives.
16. **So the sequence is not what this file first said.** Fix the address documents → file GST now → current account lands in parallel → furnish bank details to GSTN after registration. That saves roughly a week.

### 9.5 Recommendation
17. **Register voluntarily, and file this week — do not wait for the bank account.** Reasons, in order: it recovers the 18% you are currently burning on every US platform; it removes the Section 24(iii) risk entirely instead of leaving it to be classified; it removes any procurement friction with a clinic's accountant; it takes two to three weeks, so starting now means it lands before a SkinFit contract realistically would.
18. **Do not wait for the GSTIN to sign or start work.** If a contract is ready before the GSTIN is, sign it with "fees exclusive of GST and other applicable taxes as applicable" and invoice without tax until the registration date. That is clean, legal, and requires no renegotiation.
