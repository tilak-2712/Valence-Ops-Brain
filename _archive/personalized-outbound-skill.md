# Personalized Outbound — ValenceOps × Indian Elective Clinics

## What this skill does

Turns a filled clinic-audit-checklist into the next message in the outbound sequence — the Day 0 DM, a 60–90 second video script, the WhatsApp bridge line, the pilot offer, a silence follow-up, or a decline response — using one buyer psychology model, one offer-engineering formula, and one risk-reversal standard, every time. Consistency in the *thinking*, variation in the *wording*, every single time.

**Hard rule: nothing goes into a draft that isn't sitting in the checklist.** No invented details, no assumed pain points, no "probably." If a needed fact is missing, ask for it or flag the gap — don't fill it with a guess.

## The buyer you're writing for

This is not a generic SMB owner. Calibrate every draft against this:

- **Doctor-first identity.** They see themselves as clinicians, not business operators. "Scale your practice," "leads," "funnel," "pipeline," "conversion" — all of this reads as someone who doesn't understand their world. Speak in patient, consult, booking, follow-up — their words, not agency words.
- **Already burned by an agency.** The single most common pattern across Indian clinic owners is having paid for marketing before and gotten fake reviews, junk leads, or a guaranteed-patient-count promise that didn't hold. Assume this history exists. Lead with de-risking, not features.
- **WhatsApp is the real operating system; Instagram is the discovery and credibility surface.** They live on IG to be *seen*, but the actual back-and-forth with patients happens on WhatsApp. Your outbound should respect that split — earn the move from IG to WhatsApp, don't assume it.
- **Trust = competence + warmth, in that order, fast.** A sharp factual observation proves competence. A human, founder-to-founder tone (not a vendor pitch) proves warmth. You need both in the first message — competence alone reads as surveillance, warmth alone reads as another empty DM.
- **Specificity is the entire credibility mechanism in this category.** Their feed is full of agencies with generic compliments and templated carousels. A vague compliment is worse than silence — it actively signals "mass-sender."
- **Never name a guaranteed number.** "100 patients in 30 days" is the exact script every scam in this space uses, and clinic owners can recognize it instantly. Naming a number is the credibility *killer* here, not the credibility builder — the opposite of how this instinct usually works elsewhere.

## The Offer Engineering Lens

Every offer at the cold stage — the pilot, the wedge service — gets run through this before it's sent:

```
                Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood
Value   =   ─────────────────────────────────────────────
                  Time Delay  ×  Effort & Risk
```

Translate each lever to this category before writing anything:

| Lever | Generic version (don't write this) | Calibrated version (write this) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dream Outcome** | "More patients" | "The patients who already messaged you and went quiet" / "the chair that's empty Tuesday afternoon" — something concrete enough they can picture it |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | A claim about your skill | The audit finding itself. A true, specific fact about *their* clinic is proof before any case study exists |
| **Time Delay** | "We'll improve your operations" | A number. "2–3 weeks." Always named. |
| **Effort & Risk** | Anything that asks them to do work or pay upfront | "On your existing backlog. We do the work. You owe nothing if it doesn't produce a result." |

**Before presenting any draft, score it 0–10 against this equation.** If it's under 7, name which specific lever is weak — don't just rewrite the prose around the same weak lever.

## Risk Reversal Standard

The cold-stage offer is never a retainer pitch. It is always:

- **Free or performance/revenue-share** — never a flat fee at this stage
- **Time-boxed** — 2–3 weeks, named explicitly
- **Run on their existing backlog or current bookings** — no new ad spend, no new task on their plate
- **A named guarantee** — "if we don't recover [specific, modest outcome], you owe nothing and we'll tell you exactly why it didn't work"

This isn't just lowering the price to zero — a free offer with no guarantee language still carries perceived risk (their time, their patient data, their reputation if it goes wrong). Name the guarantee explicitly; don't let "it's free" do all the work alone.

## Language Rules

- **Mirror their vocabulary.** Patients, consult, follow-up, booking, walk-in. Not leads, funnel, conversion, pipeline, ROI.
- **Short sentences, plain words.** If a 12-year-old wouldn't follow it, simplify it.
- **One observation, one ask per message.** Never stack two asks in the same DM — it splits attention and halves the reply rate.
- **75–125 words for the opening DM.** Long copy in a cold DM reads as a pitch, not a person.
- **No superlatives.** "Best," "#1," "guaranteed," "leading" — their own market is saturated with this exact language already; using it yourself adds you to the noise instead of cutting through it.
- **No Hinglish or informal slang unless they use it first.** Mirroring is powerful once *they've* set the register in a reply. Initiating in casual slang to a stranger can read as presumptuous rather than warm.
- **Only checklist-verified facts go in the message.** General research about clinic-owner psychology (agency-burned, doctor-first identity, etc.) informs your *tone and offer shape* — it is never something to say out loud to a specific clinic you haven't verified it for. "I noticed your last 3 comments asking about pricing never got a reply" — fine, if it's true and in the checklist. "Clinics like yours often get burned by bad agencies" — never; it's presumptuous and unearned.

## The Sequence — Three Threads

One DM, three possible outcomes. Same shape every time; wording changes per clinic.

```
DAY 0 — SEND
   The opening DM: hook + identity (1 line) + tiny ask. No pitch.
        │
        ├── THEY REPLY POSITIVELY
        │     A1. Acknowledge + send the video (optional upgrade — see below)
        │     A2. Bridge to WhatsApp: "mind if I send this over there, easier to share?"
        │     A3. The offer: one wedge service, free/rev-share, time-boxed, on their backlog
        │     A4. Ask for the call or the coffee
        │     A5. Pilot runs → result → Case Study #1
        │
        ├── NO RESPONSE, 2–3 DAYS
        │     B1. One bump — new info, or the video as a pattern-interrupt (optional upgrade)
        │           → if they reply now: jump to A2
        │           → if still silent +4–5 days: stop, no third message
        │     B2. Park it. Revisit in 60–90 days, or once Case Study #1 exists.
        │
        └── NOT INTERESTED
              C1. Accept gracefully, leave the door open, no push, no follow-up question
              C2. Same revisit list as a parked silence.
```

## Drafting each artifact

### 1 · The Day 0 DM

Structure: **hook → identity → tiny ask.** That's it. No greeting filler, no "hope you're well."

Template:
```
Saw [specific thing from the checklist — a comment, a post, a gap].
I'm [name], I work with clinics here on exactly this kind of thing.
Want me to send the quick breakdown of what I think is happening?
```

Drafting checklist:
- [ ] First line is the audit-verified hook, not a compliment
- [ ] Second line names a person, not a company/brand voice
- [ ] Third line is a tiny, easy-to-answer yes/no — not "can we hop on a call"
- [ ] Under 125 words total (this template is closer to 30–40 — that's fine, shorter is fine, never go over 125)
- [ ] Sent from a personal account, never a brand page — flag this to the user if they haven't confirmed it

### 2 · The video script (60–90 seconds)

Four beats, in order:

1. **Name the pattern**, not a greeting — open on the specific symptom (unanswered DMs, no-shows, leads going cold after the first consult). Same observation as the DM, said out loud.
2. **Why it happens** — operational reality, not blame. "It's not that anyone's dropping the ball, it's that [the channel/volume/handoff] makes it almost impossible to catch everything manually."
3. **What you'd do first** — small, specific, named. The wedge service, framed as a 2–3 week pass on their existing backlog.
4. **The ask** — "Reply and I'll tell you exactly what I'd need to get started — no cost to look."

Tone notes: one operator talking to another, not a vendor presenting. Use "I noticed" and "here's what I think is happening," not "we offer" or "our solution."

Reusable across clinics in the same category (skin/derm/cosmetology vs. dental — build one of each, not one per clinic). The personalization at this stage lives in the one-line message wrapped around the link when you send it, not in the video itself — see the earlier conversation's note on this. Be upfront that the video is pattern-based: "made this after going through a few clinics here and noticing the same thing" builds more trust than pretending it was made in the last hour.

### 3 · The WhatsApp bridge line

Only after a reply — never cold.
```
Mind if I send this over on WhatsApp, easier to share the breakdown there — what's a good number?
```

### 4 · The pilot offer message

Run it through the Offer Engineering Lens above before sending. Template:
```
Here's what I'd want to do: [wedge service] on [specific backlog/process from the checklist], for free, over the next 2–3 weeks.
If we don't recover [specific, modest, named outcome], you owe nothing and I'll show you exactly where it stalled.
No new ad spend, no new task for your team — this runs on what's already sitting there.
```

### 5 · The call / offline meet ask

```
Want to jump on a quick call this week — or if it's easier, I'm around [area], happy to swing by and walk you through it.
```
In-person works unusually well with clinic owners specifically — lean toward offering it, don't default to "call" alone.

### 6 · The silence bump (Day 2–3)

One message, one new fact, optionally the video instead of more text.
```
One more thing I noticed — [new checklist fact, not a repeat]. Happy to send the quick breakdown either way.
```
No emoji-laden "just bumping this!" — that reads as a template, not a person.

### 7 · The decline response

```
Totally get it — happy to send the breakdown anytime if it's useful later, no pressure.
```
No follow-up question. No "can I ask why?" Accept it cleanly; log for the revisit list.

## Self-Check Before Presenting Any Draft

Run every draft through this before showing it:

- [ ] **Specificity** — is there at least one fact a stranger couldn't have written? (Pulled from the checklist, not invented.)
- [ ] **Risk** — is the risk fully on ValenceOps, not the clinic, at this stage?
- [ ] **Length** — DM under 125 words? Video script under 90 seconds spoken?
- [ ] **Tone** — does this read as founder-to-founder, or as vendor-to-customer? If the latter, cut adjectives and shorten sentences until it doesn't.
- [ ] **No guaranteed numbers** — anywhere in the message?
- [ ] **One ask only** — has nothing been stacked onto the single CTA?
- [ ] **Offer Score** — if this stage involves the pilot offer, is it 7+ on the Value Equation? If not, name the weak lever explicitly to the user rather than silently patching the copy.

---

## Anti-patterns — never do these

- Fabricating a portfolio, case study, or result that doesn't exist yet
- Naming a guaranteed patient count or result number
- Stacking more than one ask in a single message
- Generic compliments ("love your page!", "amazing work!")
- Pitching the full ValenceOps service suite cold — always one wedge service
- Drafting for a brand/agency-voiced account instead of a personal one
- Using clinic-owner psychology research (agency-burned, doctor-first identity) as something said *to* the clinic, rather than something that shapes *how* you write to them
- A third follow-up message after two unanswered touches
- Pushing back on a decline, or asking why

## Worked example

**Checklist input (illustrative — "Glow & Co Skin Clinic," Indiranagar):**
- Founder-run: likely yes (doctor appears in most Reels)
- Ads: active Meta ad for an acne-scar treatment, running ~3 weeks
- Hook: a Reel from 9 days ago has 6 comments asking "price?" / "location?" — none answered
- Reviews: 64 reviews, 4.6 rating, most recent review 5 months old
- Mystery shop: DM sent Tuesday 3:40pm, no reply as of Friday
- Wedge service chosen: dead-lead reactivation

**Day 0 DM:**
```
Saw your acne-scar Reel from last week — 6 comments asking about price and location, none answered yet.
I'm Tilak, I work with skin clinics here on exactly this — leads that came in but never got a reply.
Want me to send the quick breakdown of what's likely happening?
```

**If they reply → video script opens with:** "Noticed a Reel of yours had a handful of comments asking about pricing that never got picked up — that's almost never a 'they don't care' problem, it's a volume problem…"

**Pilot offer, once on WhatsApp:**
```
Here's what I'd want to do: go through whatever's sitting in your DMs and comments from the last 60 days, find everyone who showed real interest and never got a reply, and personally follow up with them — free, over the next 2 weeks.
If that doesn't recover at least a few real bookings, you owe nothing and I'll show you exactly where it stalled.
No new ad spend, nothing new for your team to do.
```

Score this against the Value Equation: Dream Outcome (specific, named) ✓, Perceived Likelihood (their own Reel, undeniable) ✓, Time Delay (2 weeks, named) ✓, Effort (zero on their side) ✓ — roughly 9/10. That's the bar every draft from this skill should clear.
